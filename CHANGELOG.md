# Changelog

## 1.4.0

Overland maps grow up: settled country, drowned coasts and islands, rivers that run dry, realms with peoples, provinces and neighbors, ports and sea lanes, and names you can move and bend on the canvas.

**Before you update an overland map**
- **Keep This Land in 1.3.0 first** on any overland map whose land you want to keep. New overland maps use generator 3, and a land that wasn't kept is made again by it at the next open or render: its coasts, islands, rivers and ranges move, while its towns and roads stay where they were, so some may stand in the sea (each is warned about). **Reroll Places** places the generator's towns and roads again on the new land and keeps the ones you moved, renamed or changed. A kept land reads back exactly as its own generator made it.
- On a map made before 1.4, these change at the next render even with a kept land:
  - Realm color is a band along each border, not a wash; borders wander a little and are drawn by kind (along rivers and crests, a frontier facing the wilds), so they move a little, and a realm's rank can change.
  - Every season is repainted, summer too: forests by kind, a lighter river ink with a dark casing, lakes colored by depth, the sea's shelf; a winter map gets frozen lakes, pack ice and bare woods.
  - Fields and woods follow the towns and roads, and the key shows the land's covers.
  - Landmarks (falls, passes, volcanoes, springs, sea stacks, a great tree) are drawn on every overland map.
  - Realm maps get province lines and named wild lands.
  - The names move from text drawings to AtlasScribe's labels layer: the drawings are taken out, and whether each was shown to players carries over, but a drawing you moved or edited in Foundry goes back to where the map puts it.
  - Unnamed temples may take another name (a Shrine or a Chapel where there was an Abbey).
- Points of interest (at Some) are placed on an older plan when its places are placed again, or its land is made again; set Points of Interest to None first to leave them off.
- Deserts by their ground, beaches and cliffs, rock colors and oases come with generator 3 lands only, so a kept older land doesn't get them.

**The land**
- Drowned coasts: valleys flood into inlets, ridges become headlands, hilltops islands; skerries off steep coasts, deltas and estuaries at river mouths, spits and lagoons on low coasts, each named.
- Archipelagos and worlds made of placed islands and continents, with straits and an open ocean.
- Rivers lose water crossing dry country and run out in salt pans, seasonal lakes and marshes, their beds dashed where they run dry; oases in the deserts.
- Rock by province (limestone, sandstone, granite, basalt), painted where it's bare; deserts by their ground: sand seas with dunes, stony plains, banded badlands.
- The sea: a shelf with a break at its edge, turquoise over warm shallows, reefs, pack ice by the season, beaches and cliffs.
- Seasons: snow by height and latitude, winter's frozen lakes and bare woods, autumn colors, spring floods.
- Settled land: fields and pasture around the towns and along the roads, woods cut into named forests, meadows along rivers, all in the key.

**Places and routes**
- Points of interest (a Points of Interest setting): inns, mines, quarries, watchtowers, forts, shrines, monasteries, lighthouses, customs houses, battlefields, standing stones, barrows, shipwrecks, lairs, caves, groves, brigands, pirates and monsters, where they'd be, each with a short note for you.
- The secret ones (lairs, monsters, brigands, pirates, caves) are on a tile of their own that players never see, and stay out of the key.
- Ports with an anchor, sea lanes between them, ferries over straits, caravan routes on continents and worlds, trails out to lonely places on a kingdom or a realm.

**Realms and peoples**
- Cultures: peoples grown from the oldest good land, each naming the places on its ground in its own way (its own tongue on an invented map), or by a name set you give it.
- Realms by kind (sea, river, highland, nomad, land), with Realm Sizes and Realm Growth; forms of rule (monarchy, republic, league, horde, free city) in their names; relations with their neighbors (allies, rivals, enemies, vassals); provinces named for their seats; borders by kind (along rivers and crests, disputed between enemies, a frontier facing the wilds); named wild lands; islands held across the water.
- Keep the Realms and Keep the Cultures.

**Names on the canvas**
- Overland names are on AtlasScribe's own labels layer: area names curve along their land and space out to span it, river and range names bend with their course. On the AtlasScribe Labels and Views control: drag a name to move it, double-click to edit it (text, size, turn, bend, spacing, color, shown to players), right-click to show or hide it; Tab picks a name, the arrow keys move it, [ and ] bend it, Enter edits it. Names you change stay through later updates; Put Back lays one out again. A name changed there changes only the scene; rename a place or realm in the window to change it everywhere.
- Political and cultural views: realms in flat color with provinces and capitals, or each people's land and name, switched from the same control.

**Play**
- Make This Town hands over what the map decided: walls, a temple, a city's citadel, and in words whether it's a port, how many live there, and its realm, province and people.
- Troubles (Plan Settings): invasions, rebels, floods and plague, hatched on a tile only you see.
- Make Gazetteer: a journal entry for you alone with a page per realm, the towns and their facts, the points of interest and the secrets, and map notes on the scene's towns.

## 1.3.0

Overland maps: a kingdom, a continent or a whole world, its land made by simulation and painted like a hand-painted atlas, with its towns, roads, realms and names.

**The land**
- Quick Start's Region tab has four new scales: Kingdom (240 by 160 miles, 6 mile hexes), Realm (720 by 480), Continent (2,400 by 1,600) and World (7,200 by 4,800). The region of earlier versions is the Local scale.
- The land is simulated: ranges rise where plates meet, rivers wear valleys into it, the side of a range the wind comes from gets the rain, rivers join on their way to the sea and never split, lakes fill their basins or dry to salt flats, and warmth and rain decide what grows.
- Nine kinds of land (continents, one great continent, many islands, an island, an island arc, a coast, no sea, a peninsula, an inland sea), with mountains, age, plates, coast, climate, hemisphere, wind, rain, rivers, lakes, plateaus and volcanoes to choose. A world runs pole to pole unless told otherwise.
- The same plan gives the same land every time.
- Painted like a hand-painted map: forest, grassland, heath, desert, snow on the high peaks, calm lowlands, shallows and deep sea, with a frame, a compass rose, a scale bar in miles, a key and a title cartouche.

**Towns, roads, realms and names**
- Towns, villages, castles and ruins where people would build them (river mouths, sheltered harbors, good farmland, river crossings, the foot of a pass), with highways, roads, tracks, bridges and fords between them, drawn as atlas markers. Reroll Places places them again, keeping any you moved, renamed or changed.
- Realms, if you want them: ruled from the biggest cities and towns, their borders grown along ranges, big rivers and coasts, drawn as dashed borders over a wash of color with a crown on each capital. Change a realm's name, color or capital; Reroll Realms keeps the ones you changed.
- Names for every place, sea, bay, lake, river, range, forest, island and realm, in a style per map: the English countryside, English places with older-sounding names for the land's rivers, hills and seas, or one invented tongue. Keep the Names writes them into the plan.
- Name Sets: your own naming rules as a short block of text (sounds or example names, names to use, word parts used where the land shows them, a pattern for each kind of place or feature, and what to avoid), kept in the world and copied into each map that uses one.
- Make This Town: a place's Settlement Quick Start filled in from the map (its size, name, river, coast or lake and which side it's on, its roads and the way each runs, its hills), with Follow the Map's Directions to keep them. A castle's Make This Castle makes its battle map. On a local region, Make This Town replaces Make its map.

**The window**
- A Land group in Plan Settings with every land setting and Reroll Land. When the land changes, the towns, roads and realms the generator placed are placed again on it, and one Undo puts both back.
- Keep This Land saves a land's heights in the output folder's `lands` folder and reads them back exactly, so later versions of AtlasScribe never move its coasts, ranges or rivers (and it draws faster). Forget the Kept Land lets the generator make it again.
- Shape the Land tools: a mountain range drawn along its spine, a peak or volcano, and areas of plateau, basin, lowland, land, sea, hard rock, climate and biome, which the generator follows and blends in. A biome zone with The World Reacts changes the rain and warmth there too. A river you draw is cut into the land.
- The Layer Views setting adds a view switch over overland maps: shading, uplift, rain, temperature, water flow, biomes, plates and where towns score best.
- Places (on local regions too) and painted peaks can be dragged in the window; the roads that end at a place come along, and a realm's crown follows its capital.

**The scene**
- A grid of hexes or squares in miles. Towns and roads, realms, the key and the map dressing are tiles of their own, to hide or show. Names are text drawings, hidden until you show them, white or in map ink (Label Look).

**Towns**
- Quick Start's Water has a sea coast and a lake shore for a village, hamlet or town: the place sits back from the water, its main road along the shore, with a pier and boats at its landing.

**Everywhere**
- Labels with several words are in title case: buttons, field labels, menu choices, tabs, window titles and settings. Made names never start with a lowercase "the". Scene levels, regions and the animals' folder are renamed to match, and older scenes update.

## 1.2.1

- An animal's token shows a Forgotten Adventures token through FA Nexus when it has one (a riding horse, a donkey or pony for a mule, an ox), at FA's own scale, so it matches the map's art; else the compendium creature's own token art; else the animal drawn from above, as before. FA Nexus is asked only with the Art from FA Nexus setting on, and not when Draw whole battle maps is on. A plain stand-in actor takes the FA token as its picture too.
- `snapshot` draws a token's picture at its texture's scale, fitted as Foundry fits it.

## 1.2.0

A battle map's animals are tokens.

- Horses, mules and oxen are placed as tokens, visible to everyone and on no side (neutral), drawn from above at the size the map would paint them. Each is a token of a creature from your own compendiums when one matches (the game system's packs first, then modules', then the world's: a riding horse, a mule, an ox and other names they go by), imported once into the actors' "AtlasScribe animals" folder and used again after; with no match, a plain actor of the system's creature type with no stats stands in. Delete an animal's actor there to have it looked for again. AtlasScribe ships no creatures.
- An Update replaces them as it does furniture, but leaves a token you've put in a combat or linked to an actor, and doesn't make that animal again. A scene published from a map's path keeps the ones it had.
- With the Movable props on battle maps setting off they're painted into the map, as before.
- `publish` also gives the count `animals`.

## 1.1.0

Battle maps that read better and do more, from rebuilding a hand-made scene from a plan: sunken roads, height edges that show, moonlit nights, your own lights and regions, props you can move, and a way to see a scene without opening it.

**Height**
- Sunken roads: a road's Sunken setting (feet) cuts it below the ground with a bank or cliff each side and a region in the cut; its floor follows the rises and hollows it runs through, and a stream crossing it runs under the road. Quick Start's Sunken road feature, and "a sunken road" or "a holloway" in the Describe box.
- Cliff and bank faces are painted in tiers with a shadow at the foot, over the roads that meet them, so a 10 ft drop reads at a glance. In winter a cliff's rock shows through the snow.
- Rises and hollows of one height that touch are one piece of ground, with no edge between them.
- A stretch of a rise's or hollow's edge can be another kind: `edges: [{ from, to, edge }]` in a plan, by its corners (a slope in a cliff for a ramp or a breach). Reshaping keeps each stretch on its corners.

**The scene**
- Night is moonlit: a blue darkness with enough light to see by, fires and lamps warm against it. Dark night (a new Time of day, "moonless" or "pitch dark" in the Describe box) is the near-black night of 1.0.
- Lights of your own: the Light tool, with bright and dim reach, color, strength, flicker, and lit only in the dark.
- Regions of your own: Difficult and Greater difficult terrain and Squeeze cost movement; Hazard, Cover and Note are named and colored. Each can carry a note the GM sees when a token enters.

**Props**
- Carts, wagons, sledges, boats, barrels, crates, bedrolls, tents and animals are tiles the GM can move, turn and resize, replaced on an Update as furniture is. Turning off the Movable props on battle maps setting (on by default) paints them into the map instead.
- Horses, mules and oxen, and a Picket line tool: a rope from stake to stake.
- Trails: footprints, hoofprints, paw prints or wheel ruts along a path you draw.
- A prop's Size is its whole length in feet along its longest side (a wagon's with its shafts), and at a Turn of 0 a cart, wagon, boat, bedroll or animal runs down the map, a wagon's shafts and an animal's head to the south (a sledge lies across it). Ribbons are bolder and tracks bigger.
- Winter hedges read as hedges: a snowy ridge with a shadow.

**Checks and the window**
- The plan check warns when a cart, wagon, tent, animal or other movable prop sits on a rise's or hollow's bank or cliff or on a sunken road's bank (a walkable slope is fine), or reaches into a building, and about a sunken street or a sunken road off a battle map, a light, region, trail or picket line off a battle map, a light whose dim reach is under its bright, and a region whose outline crosses itself.
- The summary lists a plan's regions with their notes, its lights, trails and picket lines.

**For scripts**
- `snapshot(scene, { side, tokens, lighting, save })`: a picture of a scene without viewing it, its lighting AtlasScribe's own approximation of Foundry's.
- `planOf(scene)` and `savePlan(plan, { file })`: publishing keeps the plan a scene was made from in the plans folder, under its id.
- `publish` also gives the counts `props` and `zones`.

**Maps that change**
- Battle maps with cliffs, banks, hedges in winter, tracks or ribbons draw differently, and a battle map's carts, barrels, tents and the rest come out of the map image as tiles (unless the setting is off).
- Quick Start's roadside wagon or cart lies along the building's front, clear of its walls and door.
- A scene set at night is now moonlit, an existing one too when it's updated; set Dark night for the old look.

**Known limits**
- Drawn animals are simple shapes: Forgotten Adventures has no animal map art for them yet.
- A wagon is a tile only, with no walls. A snapshot's lighting is close to Foundry's, not the same.
- Export as Universal VTT carries the map image, which has no movable props or furniture.
- Settlement maps' river banks and quays are unchanged: the new faces are for battle maps.

## 1.0.0

Everything that was left, in one release: new kinds of map (caves, dungeons, regions), imports from other tools, new terrain, the window's files and keyboard, and every open item from the 0.9.0 review. Plans made before still open and work, but the same plan and seed may now draw differently in places (listed under Maps that change).

**New maps**
- Caves (rock carved out by cellular automata, with openness from tight to open) and dungeons (rooms and corridors, with doors), both with floor wear: rubble, moss, puddles. On Quick Start's Battle map tab as the Cave and Dungeon sites, and in the Describe box ("a damp cave", "a dungeon with narrow corridors").
- Region maps, miles across: towns, villages, castles, ruins, towers, temples, inns and mines, the roads between them, woods, hills, mountains, marsh and the sea or a lake. Quick Start's Region tab makes one; pick a place on it and Make its map starts that place's own plan. Their scenes have a grid in miles.
- The ink style for town maps: the illustrated drawing in black ink on pale paper.
- Hills, mountains and marsh areas on town and battle maps: hills shaded and ringed with contours, mountains steep with crags, marsh with reeds and pools. Quick Start's High ground choice puts hills, or mountains with hills before them, along an edge.
- City greens and formal gardens, and a village or town green with a pond, a well, a maypole or a cross. A formal garden is built from parts (its walks, center, beds, edging and trees), each a field you can set. Maypoles and sundials as props.
- Woods near a town are cleared toward it (scattered trees, stumps, logs and woodpiles), thickening farther off. A woods field, Cleared near town, sets it.
- River shallows and islands, as two fields of a river or stream. Quick Start rivers get them.
- A castle can have a moat (the Moat choice, or "a moated castle"); the map grows 12 squares each way for it.

**Imports and exports**
- Import opens a Watabou city or village (its JSON) as a starting plan, or a Universal VTT file (.dd2vtt, .uvtt, .df2vtt) with its walls and image under the sketch.
- Export as Universal VTT, from a scene's menu in the sidebar or `api.exportUVTT`.
- Open scene's plan, from a scene's menu in the sidebar.
- Three example plans in `examples/`: a village, a city and an inn.

**The window**
- A Files view: each plan's maps and backups, their sizes, and which one its scene uses.
- Redo (Ctrl+Y), Undo (Ctrl+Z), and the Delete key removes the picked feature.
- A draft is saved as you work and offered again after a reload or crash.
- An as-built summary to copy into your notes, and what changed since the last Make scene.
- The feature list's names are buttons, with an Everything view so every feature can be reached by keyboard; Reroll and Lock have labels for screen readers; Quick Start's tabs work with the arrow keys.
- A big plan (over about 15,000 plots) says how long it will take before it's drawn.
- The notes from the last Make scene stay in the window.
- The Art window keeps your unsaved edits when another GM saves (with Use theirs), marks unsaved tabs, asks before closing on them, and shows small thumbnails.

**Settings**
- Backups: every update, the first update of a session, or never; and a Backup folder and a Cache folder of their own.
- In Plan settings, Image file: WebP or JPEG (the default picks JPEG for maps over 24 megapixels, which saves several times faster), with its quality.
- Largest image: a map bigger than this is also saved in parts and shown as tiles, for players whose graphics card can't take the whole picture.
- Batch tag, Render in a worker, Art from FA Nexus, and Art order.

**For scripts**
- New API calls: `build`, `art`, `summary`, `batch`, `importPlan`, `exportUVTT`, `openPlan`, `openFiles`. `test` is removed.
- `cancelled` is now `canceled` (on an error thrown by a canceled job); the old name still works in this release.

**Fixes from the 0.9.0 review**
- Notices and tooltips escape names from plans and tokens. Thumbnails and titles get fresh addresses, so an old one isn't shown from the cache.
- The painter frees images and canvases when a paint fails, says plainly when the browser can't give a canvas that big, and the checker's size warning estimates the memory a map needs. Art is held within a memory budget, and huge images are read by their headers first.
- FA Nexus: its window opens once per render, not once per round; files download a few at a time; when an FA Nexus update breaks the link you get a note to report instead of plain colors. Premium files on disk aren't used after a Patreon login lapses.
- Art folders: a folder inside another is read once, a folder name with ":" works, a broken file no longer makes the next render read every folder, and Cancel stops the first read.
- Number settings have upper limits, the inspector refuses fractions where a whole number is needed, and plan ids are kept to a sensible length.
- The Describe boxes read "not on a river", "unwalled", "no water", "dry", a name starting with "the", "by day", "never had a cellar", "great" (but not "great hall"), a vault inside a building, "wide corridors", and words inside a quoted name.
- Many speedups for big maps: plot lookups, street checks, back lanes and district outlines.

**Maps that change**
The same plan and seed can now draw differently:
- tree turns and small drift (a new hash in the battle and illustrated styles);
- wagons and carts kept clear of walls, side streets painted under the main road, mill races under the river, logs and rocks off gate tracks;
- row houses capped in length and cleaned of spikes; districts clipped to the map before filling;
- a gatehouse on every street through a town wall, water gates where a river passes one, a track to each mill;
- "woodland" around a place is mostly woods; inns of 24 squares or more get an upper floor;
- formal parks are picked from more designs and greens get a feature;
- a street's notable building gets a full interior; zoomed battle maps keep the overview's landmarks, palisades and cliffs;
- a river below a meeting is as wide as the two together; cave walls and wear are traced anew;
- a Battle map tab plan's road, cliff, stream, pond, rises, woods, street and scatter each have a stream of numbers of their own; snow on drawn battle roofs repeats without seams;
- a script's street pass with no spacing keeps its short dead ends.

**Other**
- Battle maps draw fields (one per area, its crop in rows by season), farmyards and docks, and a bare square as its ground rather than flagstones. A palisade on a battle map gets Foundry walls.
- D&D Fifth Edition is supported, tested in a dnd5e 6.0 world: banks, slopes, shallows and ice are its own difficult terrain, the ground is laid as floors so its falling doesn't take a token on a rise for falling (laid even with Battle map rules on Foundry's own; not for the ground with Battle map heights off), and the climb card has an Athletics check button. A jump off a cliff lands below without the system's falling damage.
- Said in notes: rooms asked for that don't fit, a cemetery, watermill or windmill asked for with no room, and an outline, water or river form Quick Start couldn't use. "0 bedrooms" means none.
- Adding a feature on the Battle map tab (a stream, say) no longer moves the rest, only what stood in its way.
- Faster metropolis plans; town maps' roofs and branches drawn with round line ends again; drawn snow on battle roofs lies in patches.
- The checker no longer warns about class-only fields on other classes (a pond's banks, say), and says when a building names more rooms than are laid out.
- Lanterns and lamp posts light a warmer, less white pool.
- A wilderness cliff runs along the top or the foot of the map.

## 0.9.0

Art for everyone, and the release candidate for 1.0.0 (there was no 0.8.0). Battle maps no longer need FA Nexus or any art: they paint with Forgotten Adventures files found by name in folders on your server, with any other pack set up through a manifest, filename rules or art you choose in the new Art window, and they draw whatever has no art, in a painted or an inked look. Quick Start's Settlement tab gets a Describe box and choices that follow the size. The README is rewritten for a first-time user, with pictures, and the full reference moves to `docs/guide.md`.

**Art for everyone**
- Art folders: a new setting (Configure Settings, AtlasScribe, Art, on its Folders tab) lists folders on the server, from any file source Foundry can browse, that battle maps and the textured style paint from before FA Nexus. Until you set your own it's FA Nexus's download folder when that exists, so art FA Nexus downloaded works with FA Nexus off. Forgotten Adventures files are found by their names anywhere in a folder, and FA Nexus fetches only what the folders lack, so a map whose art is all in a folder no longer opens FA Nexus at all (and starts painting in a moment instead of several seconds). The Folders tab shows what each folder holds.
- A folder of other art can carry an `atlas-scribe.json` manifest naming which images fill which roles (files, `*` patterns or whole folders) and the art's pixels a square; art at another scale is resized to match. See the guide's Art section (`docs/guide.md`).
- Where a season's art (snowy slate roofs, say) can't be had, a battle map now paints the role's plain art instead of a plain color.
- The Art window (Configure Settings, AtlasScribe, Art, or the new pictures button in the AtlasScribe window): its Art tab lists every kind of art AtlasScribe paints, grouped, with a picture of what it's painted with now and where that comes from, and lets you choose an image or a folder of images for any of them instead, with the art's pixels a square, for its seasons and sizes too if you like; its Rules tab holds filename rules that give a role every image whose name matches a pattern (`*slate*roof*`). A role takes at most 32 variants.
- New API calls `rescanArt()` and `openArt()`.
- Furniture from a pack that isn't Forgotten Adventures keeps its own size and shape (shrunk to fit the piece if it's bigger), instead of being stretched to FA's.
- An art file that won't load but is still on the server (a damaged image, say) is left out by name until the art is read again, instead of every folder being read again at each render.
- A rule or manifest naming a role AtlasScribe doesn't paint says so; role names are matched without regard to capitals. A file in two listed folders, one inside the other, counts once.
- With FA Nexus but no Patreon login, a note says how many Forgotten Adventures files need it.
- A battle map drawn entirely because there's no art no longer ends with a notice listing what wasn't found.

**Drawn battle art**
- A battle map needs no art now: whatever has none (grass, paving, floors, roofs, water, trees, props, furniture) is drawn, shaped like the art it stands in for, instead of a plain color. Furniture and mist drawn this way are tiles you can move as usual, their images saved beside the map.
- Two looks, a world setting (Drawn battle art): Painted, shaded in natural colors (the default), or Inked, pale colors with ink lines like a town map.
- Draw whole battle maps, a world setting: every battle map drawn entirely, even where art folders or FA Nexus have art, so a map has one look (and no art is fetched).

**Settlement Quick Start**
- A Describe box, as on the Battle map tab: type what you want ("a walled port city called 'Saltmere' with a cathedral, a citadel and no slums") and what it understood shows as chips and is set in the form below, where you can change it. A chip's × takes it out; words it doesn't know are listed.
- The choices follow the size. A village's or town's center can be a well; a city's a cathedral or a palace beside its square, its ward taking the square. What a size can't have (a cathedral for a village, the sea for a town, an old wall for a city) is grayed out with why.
- Say what a village or town has: a temple, an inn, a smithy, a cemetery, a watermill, a windmill, a wall (a palisade around a village, a town wall around a town). A box left alone is as its size has it; Reset puts them all back.
- Say which quarters a city has, whether it has a citadel, and leave out its old wall, suburbs, cemeteries or mills. A quarter left out isn't outside the walls either (no poor quarter, no shanties).
- A cemetery, mill, wall or citadel asked for that finds no room is said.
- Quick Start's API takes `has`, `quarters` and `outside`. Plans you made before, and new ones left on their defaults, are the same.
- In the Describe box, "a market" is a market square, "a mill" is a watermill when the form has water, and words inside a name aren't read as choices.

**Smaller changes**
- The Campfire, Brazier, Torch, Lamp post, Cairn, Standing stone and Sledge tools are in the Draw palette (missing from it since 0.7.0).
- The battle map Describe box reads "a cellar of casks" as "In the cellar: casks", and knows "among".
- The Map dressing setting's help says the map's name goes on a hidden tile.
- A role's art chosen by hand no longer makes the Art window scroll sideways.

## 0.7.0

Battle maps. A plan at 5 feet a square is painted from Forgotten Adventures art and made a scene to fight on: buildings with walls, doors, windows, rooms and furniture under roofs that fade as a token walks in, floors above and cellars as levels, castles with a wall walk and gatehouses, height with cliffs, banks and stream beds that set a token's elevation, woods, water and winter, lights and the time of day, and the Pathfinder Second Edition rules. Make one from Quick Start's Battle map tab (or describe it in words), from an area of a town map with the Battle map tool, or by hand.

**Battle maps**
- A plan with `kind: "battle"` (5 feet a square) is now drawn as a battle map, painted from Forgotten Adventures art at its own scale: bright grass with a second grass and clover in broad patches, ground worn around the buildings, paved streets inside crisp stone curbs (square setts on streets, flagstones on a main road; cobbles and herringbone read as noise at this scale), dirt lanes, flagstone squares, wooden floors inside the buildings (stone in a temple or a smithy), stone bridges, gardens in tilled rows and one low sun casting every shadow to the northwest. A plan whose Ground is a paving or dirt rather than grass is painted in that all over.
- Its buildings are built big enough to fight in: a few houses to a street front rather than the true dozen, each district keeping its own roofs and roof forms. Plot lines are left off; instead a house gets a fenced yard behind it, square to it, with a gate in its back fence, kept clear of streets, other buildings, water and other yards. A walled yard (a churchyard, a manor's grounds) is walled around in straight runs with a gate on its street side.
- Roofs and tree crowns lie over the map as tiles of their own, so tokens can go inside: a house's roof (with its wings) fades when a token walks in under it, and a tree's crown opens around a token beneath it. A windmill's sails turn over its cap on its roof's tile. Render saves them beside the map, in a folder named after it, and Make scene (or `publish` with render's result) places them; a republish replaces AtlasScribe's own and leaves the GM's tiles alone.
- Buildings have walls. ## 0.6.0

Maps drawn as maps. Settlements and cities are now drawn in an illustrated style that needs no art (ink lines, one light palette, streets by their paving, fields by their crop), with a frame, compass rose, scale bar and a name tile you can reveal to players, and a city's busy quarters are built up in continuous rows of houses along their streets.

**The illustrated style**
- Maps are now drawn in a new illustrated style, which needs no art: one light palette with ink lines along every edge, a settlement's built-up ground as one pale mass (crisp inside a town wall, fading out into the fields elsewhere), roofs in two tones with inked eaves, streets in the tone and marks of their paving (gray cobbles with faint stones, tan dirt lanes, speckled gravel, setts, flagstones, brick and herringbone for squares), better paving running through a junction, a square's paving carried out to its streets and the house fronts around it (small corners left between them are paved too), bridges drawn as the road carried over the water between inked parapets, fields marked by their crop (close furrows on plowed land, broken rows of stubble, stippled ripe grain, grass tufts on pasture and fallow), clumped tree canopies (bare branches in winter), water with inked banks and a deeper middle (a dashed ripple down a river, lines following the shore of a lake or the sea), and a faint paper grain. Lines wander a little, as if drawn by hand. It paints a city in about five seconds.
- A plan's new Style setting picks illustrated or textured. A settlement is illustrated unless its plan says textured; a battle map (when they come) is textured. Existing plans with no style are now drawn illustrated: set Style to textured to paint them from FA Nexus as before.
- Map dressing: a new plan setting (Map dressing) adds a ruled frame with ticks every ten squares, a compass rose and a scale bar in feet (a round length about a fifth of the map across, saying what one square is) in the map's emptiest corners, and the map's name on a cartouche in another corner, as a tile of its own that Make scene adds hidden, so the GM can show it to the players when they're ready. Quick Start turns it on for a city or metropolis.
- The nobles' palace is drawn as a palace: a walled precinct entered by a gate with a pier each side, a paved court of honor with a fountain between two wings running out from the main range, square pavilions at the corners and a domed one at the middle, and formal gardens behind on the same axis (beds edged with box either side of a broad walk, a basin, rows of trees).

**Denser cities**

## 0.3.0

Quick Start: a hamlet, village or town from a few choices in the AtlasScribe window (a button in the Scenes tab), changed in a quick preview and made into a scene, with generated streets, plots, houses, fields and woods.

- The AtlasScribe window, from a button in the Scenes tab: Quick Start (a hamlet, village or town from a few choices: season, water, roads, a square or a green, and farms and woods around it; any left to the seed), the preview with rerolls, locks and reshaping, the plan's problems, saving plans in the world (the output folder's `plans` folder; the last one opens with the window), opening, importing and exporting them, and Make scene or Update scene. `api.open()` opens it; `api.quickPlan(choices)` makes a plan without it.

Reroll any district or area there, lock the ones you like, reroll the rest, undo, then copy the plan or take it from the window (`app.plan`) to render. Click a district, area, road, river or wall (on the map, or a name in the list) to show its handles, or turn on Shapes to show them all; drag a district's or area's corners to reshape it, or a road's, river's or wall's points to move it (a point where two roads meet moves for both, and bridges and fords follow to where the roads now cross the water) (a corner two districts share moves for both, and a corner dropped on another snaps to it), drag the dot in the middle of a side to add a corner (on a side two districts share, both get it), and alt-click or right-click a corner to remove it; a reshape that would make a district cross itself is refused; a circle's or ellipse's edge handle sets its size. The map redraws when you let go.
- Streets: districts now make their own streets and lanes, steered by a tensor field built from the plan's roads, rivers and squares, so they run along and across the roads and bend gently. A square with `radial: true` gets streets that ring it. District types (the district's `profile`): `hamlet` (no new streets), `village`, `town` and `core`, each with its own spacing, bend and lanes; `bend`, `spacing` and `axis` override them. Where two districts meet, their streets meet at fixed points on the shared edge, so rerolling one never moves the other. Streets cross water where it makes sense and place their own bridges and footbridges.
- Plots: houses now stand on plots cut from the blocks between streets (after Vanegas et al. 2012, the method CityEngine uses, and the burgage and toft-and-croft plots of real medieval towns and villages): a band along each street side, cut square to the street at varied widths, the wider street taking the corner, plots meeting back to back halfway to the next street. The house stands at the front (set back a little in villages, on the street line in towns and cores, where neighbors share walls), with a shed, barn or workshop behind by district type, and in hamlets and villages sometimes a croft (a small field) behind the yard: about a quarter of village plots, half way back, fenced along its sides and open at the back so it fades into the fields. A district's `crofts` (share), `croftDepth` (0.1 to 1) and `croftFence` (`all`, `sides`, `none`) change that. No plot lines are drawn by default: plots show by what stands and grows on them. A district's `plotLines: true` draws them: in hamlets and villages a faint fence, low wall or hedge around each house's yard and around a croft (never along a street), elsewhere faint walls between long plots behind the yards. Sheds and barns are small and stand across the yard, so they never read as a second house. A district's `spacious` (0 to 1) widens plots and spaces houses out. Uses Clipper2 (Boost license), bundled in `vendor/`.
- Building shapes: houses take the forms medieval town houses took on their plots: a plain block, a rear wing running back from one end (an L), two wings (a U), or, on a deep, wide plot, wings and a back range around a yard (a courtyard house or inn). Town and core plots are now and then joined into a wealthier double plot, whose house leans to the bigger forms. In towns and cores, houses in a row share a roof material, so a terrace reads as a row. Roofs are painted as real roofs: hipped (four slopes, hips to the corners) or gabled, each slope textured along its eave and shaded by which way it faces the sun; a wing's roof runs on into its house's through a pair of valleys, with no outline across the join. Village and hamlet houses are mostly hipped; terraces and sheds are gabled; a plan building can say `roofStyle`.
- Back lanes: towns now follow the burgage pattern of real medieval towns: main streets wider apart, long narrow plots running from the street back to a back lane a plot's depth behind it, a few cross lanes, and at the far end of many plots a stable, workshop or warehouse on the lane. Back lanes carry no house fronts; where one would run into a neighboring district it turns back to its own street. Corner plots on curving streets now go to the street they actually face, so cores and towns have fewer empty plots.
- Filling the open ground: the back of each plot, behind the yard, now holds a strip garden, an orchard in rows, a few trees, or open ground, by district type, and the ground no plot takes (beyond the back lanes, the middle of big blocks) gets trees scattered evenly in loose clumps (Poisson-disk sampling, thinned by a noise). Long town plots show as strips through what's in them.
- Roof forms (from the roofs in hand-drawn town maps): besides gabled and hipped roofs, houses now get half-hips (a gable with its peak clipped into a small hip), flat roofs behind a stone parapet, and in cores mansards (steep slopes around a flat top with a glass skylight). Square sheds get pyramid roofs, and now and then a shed is round, a dovecote under a cone. Ridges and hips carry a pale ridge cap, so each roof's shape reads at a glance; in winter the caps are snowy. Every roof's texture now runs downslope on each slope.
- House plans and details: besides L, U and courtyard houses, some houses have a wing from the middle of the back (a T). A house set back from the street sometimes has a bay out to the front under its own small roof (a cross-gable); a long roof sometimes has gables on its street side, wall dormers or cross-gables rising straight from the eave; a long plain house is often built in two or three sections, each with its own roof, stepping in depth and height; and a few big houses have a round corner turret under a cone. The details never change where houses stand.
- Roof colors: roofs now come in the colors of the art, not just plain slate, shingle and red or brown tile: black, blue, purple and mossy slate; red, orange, brown, tan, gray, black, blue, green, purple and mossy tile in several tile shapes; dark, light, red, ashen and walnut shingle; and thatch. Each district keeps a short palette of its own, picked from its seed by type (thatch and shingle in hamlets, slate in villages, colored slate and tile in towns), so neighboring districts can look different. A district's `roofs` and `roofStyles` (lists) set its own. In winter, colored roofs get snow on them too.
- `checkPlan` warns when a district's or area's outline crosses itself.
- Fixes: a shape less than a tenth of a square across (an area, square, building or district) is now reported by `checkPlan` instead of crashing the layout; houses and their parts never reach into a street, their own district's or a neighbor's.
- Woods grow in groves: trees close enough that their crowns touch, gathered where a noise runs high, with open ground and clearings between, holding about as many trees as the area asks for (`trees`). A building or road placed later removes only the trees it stands on.
- Parks: an area of class `park` is now drawn, by its `style`. A village green (`green`, the default): open grass, big trees in groups around the edge and a few in the open, and trodden paths from the middle of each side a road runs along to a point near the middle. A formal garden (`formal`), laid out square to the area's `rot`: gravel walks around it and across it, a round basin where they cross, four beds with clipped hedges, and avenues of trees along the long walk. Streets and plots keep out of parks.
- Rerolling and locking: `api.reroll(plan, ids)` gives a plan with new seeds for those districts or areas (or every one that isn't locked), so they're drawn again while everything else stays exactly as it was; `api.lock(plan, ids)` and `api.unlock(plan, ids)` mark the ones a reroll must leave alone. Rerolling is repeatable: the same plan rerolls the same way, and rerolling the result gives the next variation.
- Curves: roads, rivers and walls now curve smoothly through the plan's points instead of running straight between them, so a point placed by hand stays on the line and a road that ended on another still meets it. A sharp turn (over 60 degrees) on a road or river is rounded off instead, so it never swings wide of its line; a ring road or a wall around a yard curves all the way around. Walls keep their sharp corners and curve only their gentle bends. Where a curve would swing into a building the plan placed, the notes say so. `straight: true` on a road, water or wall keeps it straight (a canal, a Roman road, a town wall's runs). Districts' streets follow the curves, so a plan's districts lay out a little differently than before.
- Bridges and fords line up with their roads: a plan's crossing is moved to the nearest place a road crosses the water (each place takes one crossing), turned to run along the road, made long enough to span the water and its banks at that angle, and a bridge made as wide as its road. One with no road over water near it stays where the plan puts it, with a note.
- Summer art: maps outside winter now paint with FA Nexus art too (they were mostly plain color): grass with lighter, lusher and patchy grass in patches; dirt roads and grassy verges and yards; gravel, cobbles and stone; river, stream and pond water; leafy trees, green firs in three sizes and green hedge bushes, with yellow, red and russet trees and bushes in autumn. Spring and autumn use the summer art unless they have their own. Outside winter the grass is lifted toward a lighter green and the darkening at the map's edges is halved, since FA's grass reads dark at overview scale. Winter dirt roads are slush. Deciduous trees are drawn in leaf outside winter (bare only in winter).
- Field systems: an area of class `fields` is now drawn, filled with fields from a Voronoi diagram (d3-delaunay, ISC license, bundled in `vendor/`). Two systems, by the area's `system` (else picked from its seed): `patchwork`, enclosed fields of irregular shape running long with the lie of the area, a few merged into bigger ones, hedged (with a tree in the hedge here and there) or walled along the roads, around the system's edge and on about a quarter of the sides between fields, the rest a faint grass edge, so not every field is ringed; and `strips`, open fields in furlongs, each plowed in long narrow strips running with the land or across it, with grass balks between. About a third of the fields (or whole furlongs) are left as grass, pasture or common, so the land isn't cultivated wall to wall; `coverage` (0 to 1) sets the share that's cultivated. `border` (`hedge`, `drystone` or `none`) and `fieldSize` (about how big a field or furlong is, in feet) change them too. Each field has a crop for the season (plowed, growing, ripe, stubble, hay, pasture or fallow), painted with FA Nexus crop, hay, clover and soil art and drawn with its rows (furrows, drills, the windrows of cut hay). Fields keep off the roads, water and walls, a ragged margin of grass 75 to 200 feet wide around every district (so a village sits in its own grass rather than as a box among the crops) and 30 to 90 feet around every farmyard, a farm's lane (a track from each farmstead to the road beside it), squares, yards, buildings, other areas and any field system listed before them (where two systems meet, only the first draws a border along the edge). They come from the area's own shape and seed, so moving a road clips them differently but never reshuffles them (strips, hedgerow trees and all), and rerolling a field system redraws it alone. Scraps and slivers the roads and margins cut off are left as grass, with no balks beside them. Under snow a field is mostly the snow around it, its own texture showing only faintly. A huge area with a small `fieldSize` gets bigger fields, with a note, rather than running out of fields partway across.
- Quick Start's farmland is now field systems in the stretches of land between the roads (all but one stretch in farmland, running to the map's edge; about half in mixed country, a ragged way out from the village; now and then in woodland), in place of the field walls along the roads.
- Tracks: a district's street that stops at the district's edge now carries on as a narrow trodden track out into the land, wandering a little, until it reaches a field's gate, a road, water, a wood or other area, or its length, so streets no longer end abruptly at the edge. These tracks and the farm lanes are drawn faint; a road of class `track` in the plan is drawn as a road.
- Squares sit in their roads rather than on top of them: a worn apron around each that the roads and streets run into, the paving laid with a soft, worn edge, and the road's worn line carried faintly across it. A back lane that has just crossed a street now ends there, instead of running on and laying a link back to its own street alongside that one.
- Gardens: only a share of houses get a tended garden (the district's `gardens`, a share from 0 to 1, about one in ten by default; `true` and `false` from older plans still read), in varied sizes, and they're drawn quietly: a thin fence and faint rows in winter, soft planted rows in other seasons.
- Make scene saves the plan first (so the scene can always be updated from it later), has a Cancel button while it paints, and says when art couldn't be had and parts are painted in plain colors. A plan with no id gets one from its name with a short random tail, so two plans of the same name never share a file or a scene.
- Winter: a gravel square or road under snow is trodden slush, not near-black gravel.
- Plans: `checkPlan` now stops a plan that would take minutes to lay out (a feature reaching far past the map, or more than 100 feet a square, or a plan seed that isn't a number), reads a building's `variant`, warns about a feature's seed that isn't a number, no longer calls an outline that crosses itself too small, and says plainly that where districts overlap, both build and their houses stand on each other's. A wood with `trees: 0` is open ground, and one too big to fill says so. A district's or area's outline lays out the same whichever way around its corners run. Houses keep off a road running just outside their district, a back lane never links across into the next district, and no green's tree or neighbor's tree stands in a street. Laying out a big town is faster.
- Rendering: big maps paint faster, since a soft-edged pass now covers only the part of the map it touches and things outside a tile are skipped; a small map no longer fades toward its edges; a bush placed in a plan now shows in the full style; in the sketch, a flat roof's parapet no longer runs across a wing's join.

## 0.2.0

The engine: a map plan in, a painted map and a scene out, driven from the console (see the README's "From a script").

- Plans: `api.checkPlan(plan)` reads a plan (JSON, or a fenced `mapplan` block) and checks it, explaining each problem in plain words, errors first; a badly formed plan is reported, never crashes it. One list of feature types (roads, water, crossings, squares, buildings, landmarks, districts, areas, walls, props and labels) sets what each takes and its defaults; the image settings (`output`) and the scene settings (`output.scene`) are checked too, including Foundry's smallest grid and the largest image WebP can hold.
- Rendering: `api.render(plan)` lays the plan out and paints it in a background worker, so Foundry never freezes, with a progress bar, then saves the image without writing over anything. The first fill is the "roadside" district (houses along every road and lane through it, each with a garden behind; houses and gardens never overlap each other, a yard, a square, water or a road), plus farmsteads, woods of any shape, hedgerows and a textured winter style. Art comes from FA Nexus when it's installed, through a role map; anything missing, or everything if FA Nexus can't be used, is painted in plain colors. Big maps paint in tiles, with no visible joins.
- Seeds: the same plan always gives the same map, and each district, farm and wood has its own seed (`seeds`): rerolling one district never moves the district beside it.
- Publishing: `api.publish(plan, { image })`, or `api.render(plan, { publish: true })`, makes the map a scene: the background, the grid, lighting, fog, weather and navigation from `output.scene`, a thumbnail, and the plan's labels as locked text drawings (hidden from players unless a label says `show: "all"`; sizes small, normal, large and huge). Give `scene: <id>` to update a scene AtlasScribe made (any other needs `force: true`): the whole scene is backed up first, its name and your own drawings are kept, and only its old labels are replaced.
- `api.cancel()` stops the job that's running; one job runs at a time.
