# Changelog

## Unreleased

- Settings in the window: click anything on the map (a district, wood, field system, farm, road, river, wall, square, building, pond, bridge, prop or label) or a name in the list, and its settings open in the side panel in place of the list. Only the settings it uses are shown, each with what applies when it's left empty; a change redraws the map at once (a change made while it's still drawing waits its turn), and a setting the plan sets can be put back to its default. Less used settings are under More. Delete takes a feature out of the plan, with its seed and lock (Undo brings it back). The gear opens the plan's own settings: name, season, ground, the map's size, feet and pixels a square, and the scene's weather, darkness, light and fog.
- Drawing in the window: Draw opens a palette of tools (district, woods, fields, park, farm, yard, square, building; road, lane, track, river, stream, wall, hedge; bridge, ford, tree, well, cart, label). Districts and areas are drawn corner by corner or as a box, their corners snapping onto other districts' and areas' corners and sides and onto the map's edge; where it meets a neighbour along a side each takes the other's corners there, so the side is shared, streets meet across it and its corners move together. Roads, rivers and walls are drawn point by point, their points snapping onto a road (water onto water, a wall onto a wall) or the map's edge; a new road over water, or new water over a road, gets a bridge there (a ford for a track or path over a stream) unless one is already near. Buildings are a box or a click; bridges, fords, props and labels a click. Double-click, Enter or Finish ends a drawing, Backspace takes the last point off, Esc cancels; the new feature's settings open at once. A drawing the plan can't take (a shape that crosses itself, one far off the map) says why and nothing is added.
- `checkPlan` no longer warns that a district has no road into it when it shares a side with another district whose streets reach it through gates (a district drawn beside a town gets its streets that way). A hamlet still needs a road, since it builds only along roads, and the warning now says so.
- Ways in: a district drawn with no road into it and no neighbouring district's streets reaching it gets a lane from the nearest road it can reach without running through another district, square, building, yard or park (by a dry way if one is not much longer, else with a bridge); a square drawn off the roads inside a district gets a street from the nearest road into it the same way (a lane in a village or hamlet), so houses line the way to it. Farms, yards, parks and squares out in the country get a faint lane to the nearest road (farms already had one within 12 squares; now within 40). A lane crosses woods and fields, clearing the trees and hedge bushes in its way, but never water, a district, another yard, park or farm, a building or a pond; it never ends off the map or at the water's edge; a yard beside a farm shares the farm's lane; and where the only way in is a long way round, there's no lane. A green's paths start where its lane, a road or a district's street meets it, at most one a side and four in all, so a green inside a town opens onto its streets, and rerolling the district next door leaves the green as it was.
- The district type `core` is now called `city` ("a district built up like a city": dense rows and alleys), since a dense district needn't be in the middle. Plans that say `core` still read as `city`, as `roadside` still reads as `hamlet`.
- Pinning: click a house or a district's street or lane that the generator made, and Pin copies it into the plan, so rerolls keep it. A house becomes a `landmark` whose buildings are drawn exactly as they were (their roofs, wings, bays, turrets, chimney and the joins between roofs: buildings can now carry `chimneyAt`, `join` and `cut`); it can be named, show its name on the map, and have the roof of all its buildings changed at once. A street becomes a plan `road` along the same line (`straight`, as wide as it was; a back lane as a lane), with the bridges the district had put on it. The district fills round what's pinned when it's rerolled. Districts' streets now keep a street's width off every plan building (pinned or placed), so none clips one; this moves a few streets in plans with buildings of their own.
- Wood density: a wood's `density` (`sparse`, `open`, `thick` or `dense`) sets how thick its trees stand, so a wood drawn bigger or smaller keeps its look; a wood with neither a density nor a tree count is thick, as before, and `trees` still wins when set.
- Labels in the window: drawn at their true size, turned, in their colour, font and opacity. Click a label, then drag its box to move it or the dot beside it to turn it (Shift for steps of 15 degrees); dropped or placed on a road, river or stream it turns to run along it, never upside down. New label settings: `angle` (degrees), `color`, `font` (any font Foundry offers) and `alpha` (opacity); colour and font left empty use the plan's `output.scene.labelColor` and `labelFont`, which can now be set in the window too. A label with `of` (a district's, area's or landmark's id) and no `at` sits inside that feature, shows its name unless it has `text`, and follows it when it's reshaped; `offset` moves it off the middle. A district's or area's "Name on the map" setting adds or removes such a label, and deleting the feature takes it too. Scenes get the turn, colour, font and opacity. Moving, turning or restyling a label no longer redraws the map.
- Districts and areas can have a `name`, shown in the window and on the outlines in place of their id.
- A bridge or ford can be clicked in the window where it's drawn (moved onto its road), not only where the plan first put it.

## 0.3.0

Quick Start: a hamlet, village or town from a few choices in the AtlasScribe window (a button in the Scenes tab), changed in a quick preview and made into a scene, with generated streets, plots, houses, fields and woods.

- The AtlasScribe window, from a button in the Scenes tab: Quick Start (a hamlet, village or town from a few choices: season, water, roads, a square or a green, and farms and woods around it; any left to the seed), the preview with rerolls, locks and reshaping, the plan's problems, saving plans in the world (the output folder's `plans` folder; the last one opens with the window), opening, importing and exporting them, and Make scene or Update scene. `api.open()` opens it; `api.quickPlan(choices)` makes a plan without it.
- Preview: `api.preview(plan)` opens a window with a quick sketch of the plan in plain colours, laid out and drawn in about a second with no art, its districts and areas outlined and named. Reroll any district or area there, lock the ones you like, reroll the rest, undo, then copy the plan or take it from the window (`app.plan`) to render. Click a district, area, road, river or wall (on the map, or a name in the list) to show its handles, or turn on Shapes to show them all; drag a district's or area's corners to reshape it, or a road's, river's or wall's points to move it (a point where two roads meet moves for both, and bridges and fords follow to where the roads now cross the water) (a corner two districts share moves for both, and a corner dropped on another snaps to it), drag the dot in the middle of a side to add a corner (on a side two districts share, both get it), and alt-click or right-click a corner to remove it; a reshape that would make a district cross itself is refused; a circle's or ellipse's edge handle sets its size. The map redraws when you let go.
- Streets: districts now make their own streets and lanes, steered by a tensor field built from the plan's roads, rivers and squares, so they run along and across the roads and bend gently. A square with `radial: true` gets streets that ring it. District types (the district's `profile`): `hamlet` (no new streets), `village`, `town` and `core`, each with its own spacing, bend and lanes; `bend`, `spacing` and `axis` override them. Where two districts meet, their streets meet at fixed points on the shared edge, so rerolling one never moves the other. Streets cross water where it makes sense and place their own bridges and footbridges.
- Plots: houses now stand on plots cut from the blocks between streets (after Vanegas et al. 2012, the method CityEngine uses, and the burgage and toft-and-croft plots of real medieval towns and villages): a band along each street side, cut square to the street at varied widths, the wider street taking the corner, plots meeting back to back halfway to the next street. The house stands at the front (set back a little in villages, on the street line in towns and cores, where neighbours share walls), with a shed, barn or workshop behind by district type, and in hamlets and villages sometimes a croft (a small field) behind the yard: about a quarter of village plots, half way back, fenced along its sides and open at the back so it fades into the fields. A district's `crofts` (share), `croftDepth` (0.1 to 1) and `croftFence` (`all`, `sides`, `none`) change that. No plot lines are drawn by default: plots show by what stands and grows on them. A district's `plotLines: true` draws them: in hamlets and villages a faint fence, low wall or hedge round each house's yard and round a croft (never along a street), elsewhere faint walls between long plots behind the yards. Sheds and barns are small and stand across the yard, so they never read as a second house. A district's `spacious` (0 to 1) widens plots and spaces houses out. Uses Clipper2 (Boost licence), bundled in `vendor/`.
- Building shapes: houses take the forms medieval town houses took on their plots: a plain block, a rear wing running back from one end (an L), two wings (a U), or, on a deep, wide plot, wings and a back range round a yard (a courtyard house or inn). Town and core plots are now and then joined into a wealthier double plot, whose house leans to the bigger forms. In towns and cores, houses in a row share a roof material, so a terrace reads as a row. Roofs are painted as real roofs: hipped (four slopes, hips to the corners) or gabled, each slope textured along its eave and shaded by which way it faces the sun; a wing's roof runs on into its house's through a pair of valleys, with no outline across the join. Village and hamlet houses are mostly hipped; terraces and sheds are gabled; a plan building can say `roofStyle`.
- Back lanes: towns now follow the burgage pattern of real medieval towns: main streets wider apart, long narrow plots running from the street back to a back lane a plot's depth behind it, a few cross lanes, and at the far end of many plots a stable, workshop or warehouse on the lane. Back lanes carry no house fronts; where one would run into a neighbouring district it turns back to its own street. Corner plots on curving streets now go to the street they actually face, so cores and towns have fewer empty plots.
- Filling the open ground: the back of each plot, behind the yard, now holds a strip garden, an orchard in rows, a few trees, or open ground, by district type, and the ground no plot takes (beyond the back lanes, the middle of big blocks) gets trees scattered evenly in loose clumps (Poisson-disk sampling, thinned by a noise). Long town plots show as strips through what's in them.
- Roof forms (from the roofs in hand-drawn town maps): besides gabled and hipped roofs, houses now get half-hips (a gable with its peak clipped into a small hip), flat roofs behind a stone parapet, and in cores mansards (steep slopes round a flat top with a glass skylight). Square sheds get pyramid roofs, and now and then a shed is round, a dovecote under a cone. Ridges and hips carry a pale ridge cap, so each roof's shape reads at a glance; in winter the caps are snowy. Every roof's texture now runs downslope on each slope.
- House plans and details: besides L, U and courtyard houses, some houses have a wing from the middle of the back (a T). A house set back from the street sometimes has a bay out to the front under its own small roof (a cross-gable); a long roof sometimes has gables on its street side, wall dormers or cross-gables rising straight from the eave; a long plain house is often built in two or three sections, each with its own roof, stepping in depth and height; and a few big houses have a round corner turret under a cone. The details never change where houses stand.
- Roof colours: roofs now come in the colours of the art, not just plain slate, shingle and red or brown tile: black, blue, purple and mossy slate; red, orange, brown, tan, grey, black, blue, green, purple and mossy tile in several tile shapes; dark, light, red, ashen and walnut shingle; and thatch. Each district keeps a short palette of its own, picked from its seed by type (thatch and shingle in hamlets, slate in villages, coloured slate and tile in towns), so neighbouring districts can look different. A district's `roofs` and `roofStyles` (lists) set its own. In winter, coloured roofs get snow on them too.
- `checkPlan` warns when a district's or area's outline crosses itself.
- Fixes: a shape less than a tenth of a square across (an area, square, building or district) is now reported by `checkPlan` instead of crashing the layout; houses and their parts never reach into a street, their own district's or a neighbour's.
- Woods grow in groves: trees close enough that their crowns touch, gathered where a noise runs high, with open ground and clearings between, holding about as many trees as the area asks for (`trees`). A building or road placed later removes only the trees it stands on.
- Parks: an area of class `park` is now drawn, by its `style`. A village green (`green`, the default): open grass, big trees in groups round the edge and a few in the open, and trodden paths from the middle of each side a road runs along to a point near the middle. A formal garden (`formal`), laid out square to the area's `rot`: gravel walks round it and across it, a round basin where they cross, four beds with clipped hedges, and avenues of trees along the long walk. Streets and plots keep out of parks.
- Rerolling and locking: `api.reroll(plan, ids)` gives a plan with new seeds for those districts or areas (or every one that isn't locked), so they're drawn again while everything else stays exactly as it was; `api.lock(plan, ids)` and `api.unlock(plan, ids)` mark the ones a reroll must leave alone. Rerolling is repeatable: the same plan rerolls the same way, and rerolling the result gives the next variation.
- Curves: roads, rivers and walls now curve smoothly through the plan's points instead of running straight between them, so a point placed by hand stays on the line and a road that ended on another still meets it. A sharp turn (over 60 degrees) on a road or river is rounded off instead, so it never swings wide of its line; a ring road or a wall round a yard curves all the way round. Walls keep their sharp corners and curve only their gentle bends. Where a curve would swing into a building the plan placed, the notes say so. `straight: true` on a road, water or wall keeps it straight (a canal, a Roman road, a town wall's runs). Districts' streets follow the curves, so a plan's districts lay out a little differently than before.
- Bridges and fords line up with their roads: a plan's crossing is moved to the nearest place a road crosses the water (each place takes one crossing), turned to run along the road, made long enough to span the water and its banks at that angle, and a bridge made as wide as its road. One with no road over water near it stays where the plan puts it, with a note.
- Summer art: maps outside winter now paint with FA Nexus art too (they were mostly plain colour): grass with lighter, lusher and patchy grass in patches; dirt roads and grassy verges and yards; gravel, cobbles and stone; river, stream and pond water; leafy trees, green firs in three sizes and green hedge bushes, with yellow, red and russet trees and bushes in autumn. Spring and autumn use the summer art unless they have their own. Outside winter the grass is lifted toward a lighter green and the darkening at the map's edges is halved, since FA's grass reads dark at overview scale. Winter dirt roads are slush. Deciduous trees are drawn in leaf outside winter (bare only in winter).
- Field systems: an area of class `fields` is now drawn, filled with fields from a Voronoi diagram (d3-delaunay, ISC licence, bundled in `vendor/`). Two systems, by the area's `system` (else picked from its seed): `patchwork`, enclosed fields of irregular shape running long with the lie of the area, a few merged into bigger ones, hedged (with a tree in the hedge here and there) or walled along the roads, round the system's edge and on about a quarter of the sides between fields, the rest a faint grass edge, so not every field is ringed; and `strips`, open fields in furlongs, each ploughed in long narrow strips running with the land or across it, with grass balks between. About a third of the fields (or whole furlongs) are left as grass, pasture or common, so the land isn't cultivated wall to wall; `coverage` (0 to 1) sets the share that's cultivated. `border` (`hedge`, `drystone` or `none`) and `fieldSize` (about how big a field or furlong is, in feet) change them too. Each field has a crop for the season (ploughed, growing, ripe, stubble, hay, pasture or fallow), painted with FA Nexus crop, hay, clover and soil art and drawn with its rows (furrows, drills, the windrows of cut hay). Fields keep off the roads, water and walls, a ragged margin of grass 75 to 200 feet wide round every district (so a village sits in its own grass rather than as a box among the crops) and 30 to 90 feet round every farmyard, a farm's lane (a track from each farmstead to the road beside it), squares, yards, buildings, other areas and any field system listed before them (where two systems meet, only the first draws a border along the edge). They come from the area's own shape and seed, so moving a road clips them differently but never reshuffles them (strips, hedgerow trees and all), and rerolling a field system redraws it alone. Scraps and slivers the roads and margins cut off are left as grass, with no balks beside them. Under snow a field is mostly the snow round it, its own texture showing only faintly. A huge area with a small `fieldSize` gets bigger fields, with a note, rather than running out of fields partway across.
- Quick Start's farmland is now field systems in the stretches of land between the roads (all but one stretch in farmland, running to the map's edge; about half in mixed country, a ragged way out from the village; now and then in woodland), in place of the field walls along the roads.
- Tracks: a district's street that stops at the district's edge now carries on as a narrow trodden track out into the land, wandering a little, until it reaches a field's gate, a road, water, a wood or other area, or its length, so streets no longer end abruptly at the edge. These tracks and the farm lanes are drawn faint; a road of class `track` in the plan is drawn as a road.
- Squares sit in their roads rather than on top of them: a worn apron round each that the roads and streets run into, the paving laid with a soft, worn edge, and the road's worn line carried faintly across it. A back lane that has just crossed a street now ends there, instead of running on and laying a link back to its own street alongside that one.
- Gardens: only a share of houses get a tended garden (the district's `gardens`, a share from 0 to 1, about one in ten by default; `true` and `false` from older plans still read), in varied sizes, and they're drawn quietly: a thin fence and faint rows in winter, soft planted rows in other seasons.
- Make scene saves the plan first (so the scene can always be updated from it later), has a Cancel button while it paints, and says when art couldn't be had and parts are painted in plain colours. A plan with no id gets one from its name with a short random tail, so two plans of the same name never share a file or a scene.
- Winter: a gravel square or road under snow is trodden slush, not near-black gravel.
- Plans: `checkPlan` now stops a plan that would take minutes to lay out (a feature reaching far past the map, or more than 100 feet a square, or a plan seed that isn't a number), reads a building's `variant`, warns about a feature's seed that isn't a number, no longer calls an outline that crosses itself too small, and says plainly that where districts overlap, both build and their houses stand on each other's. A wood with `trees: 0` is open ground, and one too big to fill says so. A district's or area's outline lays out the same whichever way round its corners run. Houses keep off a road running just outside their district, a back lane never links across into the next district, and no green's tree or neighbour's tree stands in a street. Laying out a big town is faster.
- Rendering: big maps paint faster, since a soft-edged pass now covers only the part of the map it touches and things outside a tile are skipped; a small map no longer fades toward its edges; a bush placed in a plan now shows in the full style; in the sketch, a flat roof's parapet no longer runs across a wing's join.

## 0.2.0

The engine: a map plan in, a painted map and a scene out, driven from the console (see the README's "From a script").

- Plans: `api.checkPlan(plan)` reads a plan (JSON, or a fenced `mapplan` block) and checks it, explaining each problem in plain words, errors first; a badly formed plan is reported, never crashes it. One list of feature types (roads, water, crossings, squares, buildings, landmarks, districts, areas, walls, props and labels) sets what each takes and its defaults; the image settings (`output`) and the scene settings (`output.scene`) are checked too, including Foundry's smallest grid and the largest image WebP can hold.
- Rendering: `api.render(plan)` lays the plan out and paints it in a background worker, so Foundry never freezes, with a progress bar, then saves the image without writing over anything. The first fill is the "roadside" district (houses along every road and lane through it, each with a garden behind; houses and gardens never overlap each other, a yard, a square, water or a road), plus farmsteads, woods of any shape, hedgerows and a textured winter style. Art comes from FA Nexus when it's installed, through a role map; anything missing, or everything if FA Nexus can't be used, is painted in plain colours. Big maps paint in tiles, with no visible joins.
- Seeds: the same plan always gives the same map, and each district, farm and wood has its own seed (`seeds`): rerolling one district never moves the district beside it.
- Publishing: `api.publish(plan, { image })`, or `api.render(plan, { publish: true })`, makes the map a scene: the background, the grid, lighting, fog, weather and navigation from `output.scene`, a thumbnail, and the plan's labels as locked text drawings (hidden from players unless a label says `show: "all"`; sizes small, normal, large and huge). Give `scene: <id>` to update a scene AtlasScribe made (any other needs `force: true`): the whole scene is backed up first, its name and your own drawings are kept, and only its old labels are replaced.
- `api.cancel()` stops the job that's running; one job runs at a time.
