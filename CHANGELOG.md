# Changelog

## Unreleased

**Battle maps**
- A plan with `kind: "battle"` (5 feet a square) is now drawn as a battle map, painted from Forgotten Adventures art at its own scale: bright grass with a second grass and clover in broad patches, ground worn around the buildings, paved streets inside crisp stone curbs (square setts on streets, flagstones on a main road; cobbles and herringbone read as noise at this scale), dirt lanes, flagstone squares, wooden floors inside the buildings (stone in a temple or a smithy), stone bridges, gardens in tilled rows and one low sun casting every shadow to the northwest.
- Its buildings are built big enough to fight in: a few houses to a street front rather than the true dozen, each district keeping its own roofs and roof forms. Plot lines are left off; instead a house gets a fenced yard behind it, square to it, with a gate in its back fence, kept clear of streets, other buildings, water and other yards. A walled yard (a churchyard, a manor's grounds) is walled around in straight runs with a gate on its street side.
- Roofs and tree crowns lie over the map as tiles of their own, so tokens can go inside: a house's roof (with its wings) fades when a token walks in under it, and a tree's crown opens around a token beneath it. Render saves them beside the map, in a folder named after it, and Make scene (or `publish` with render's result) places them; a republish replaces AtlasScribe's own and leaves the GM's tiles alone.
- Buildings have walls. They're painted into the map (stone for a temple or a tower, timber and plaster for a house, plain boards for a shed or stable, stone and timber for a smithy) with Foundry walls on top, so tokens can't see or walk through them. Each building gets a front door on the side nearest a street and a back door into its yard (and a door into its courtyard if it has one): Foundry doors that swing open, using the door art Foundry itself ships, drawn as thick as the wall (a temple's wide door swings open as two leaves). Windows along the walls let a token see out, or in, from 10 feet or closer.
- Fences and low walls stop movement but not sight, and a yard's gate is a door in its fence.
- Roofs show from the street. Each building gets a roof region at its roof's height (10 feet, or 20 for a tower or a tall building such as a temple) with Foundry's Define Surface behavior, so a token outside sees the roof, more of it from farther away, though the walls block its sight of the rooms below. A roof also clears where a token sees in through an open door or a window, and fades as before when a token walks in under it. The scene's level reaches up to the roofs, so the door art lies under them.
- Buildings stand at least a square apart, a house and its own shed too, so there's always room to walk between two. A plot too narrow for a house three squares across is left as a yard. Battle maps from earlier plans draw differently.
- A battle map's scene has token vision and fog exploration on unless the plan turns them off, so ground a token has seen stays on the map, dimmed, instead of going black once it's out of sight. A republish replaces AtlasScribe's own walls and roof regions and leaves the GM's alone (republishing it as a settlement map takes them out). The level's top is set to the roof height only when the level has none (and taken off again if the scene is republished as a settlement map), so a top the GM set is kept.
- New Battle map tool in the window: on a town or city map, drag a box over the area to fight in, or click to place one 30, 45 or 60 squares a side. The window shows its size in battle squares as you drag, amber past 60 squares a side (it can still be made). Make battle map opens a battle map of that area as a new plan, not saved until you save it. It's drawn twice true size by default, so there's room to fight; the Zoom picker (also the client setting Battle map zoom) makes it true size or one and a half times instead.
- A battle map made this way keeps the town map's streets, roads, rivers, lakes, squares (with their market stalls and fountain or cross), bridges and notable buildings (a temple, an inn, a smithy, a district's centerpiece, the plan's own buildings) where they are, grown with the zoom. Its districts keep their character and roofs but are built again at battle size, so their ordinary houses are new. A centerpiece's precinct, cloister garth and lawns stay open grass, with its wall and fountains. Houses come only where a street in the box reaches them. The new plan remembers where it came from but is independent: changing either map leaves the other alone.
- Battle maps build up the middles of their blocks behind the street fronts: back houses with yards, sheds beside them, kitchen gardens, and yards with carts, barrels and firewood (a dense quarter's own stores or workshops where it has them). Open ground away from the plots, such as a temple's precinct, stays open.
- A battle map paints a quarter's own ground (a market paved, a crafts quarter's dirt, a town's homes worn as a city's are), and a bridge has a low stone parapet along each side (a footbridge, wooden rails), which stops movement but not sight.
- For plan writers: a battle map is drawn in its own style whatever its plan's `style` says. A layout now carries `kind` and `seed`, a building its `group` (the parts that stand as one building: a house and its wings, a centerpiece's or a pinned house's parts that touch) and a main house `main`, and each building's walls as `shell` items (wall, door and window pieces); render's result has `overheads`, and publish's result `overheads`, `walls` and `roofs` (how many tiles, walls and roof regions it placed). A district's new `uses` (true by default; off, its houses are all plain), a plan's `source` (the plan, box and zoom a battle map was made from), a yard area with surface `grass` (left unworn on a battle map), and `zoomPlan` (core/zoom.mjs, also the worker's `zoom` job).

## 0.6.0

Maps drawn as maps. Settlements and cities are now drawn in an illustrated style that needs no art (ink lines, one light palette, streets by their paving, fields by their crop), with a frame, compass rose, scale bar and a name tile you can reveal to players, and a city's busy quarters are built up in continuous rows of houses along their streets.

**The illustrated style**
- Maps are now drawn in a new illustrated style, which needs no art: one light palette with ink lines along every edge, a settlement's built-up ground as one pale mass (crisp inside a town wall, fading out into the fields elsewhere), roofs in two tones with inked eaves, streets in the tone and marks of their paving (gray cobbles with faint stones, tan dirt lanes, speckled gravel, setts, flagstones, brick and herringbone for squares), better paving running through a junction, a square's paving carried out to its streets and the house fronts around it (small corners left between them are paved too), bridges drawn as the road carried over the water between inked parapets, fields marked by their crop (close furrows on plowed land, broken rows of stubble, stippled ripe grain, grass tufts on pasture and fallow), clumped tree canopies (bare branches in winter), water with inked banks and a deeper middle (a dashed ripple down a river, lines following the shore of a lake or the sea), and a faint paper grain. Lines wander a little, as if drawn by hand. It paints a city in about five seconds.
- A plan's new Style setting picks illustrated or textured. A settlement is illustrated unless its plan says textured; a battle map (when they come) is textured. Existing plans with no style are now drawn illustrated: set Style to textured to paint them from FA Nexus as before.
- Map dressing: a new plan setting (Map dressing) adds a ruled frame with ticks every ten squares, a compass rose and a scale bar in feet (a round length about a fifth of the map across, saying what one square is) in the map's emptiest corners, and the map's name on a cartouche in another corner, as a tile of its own that Make scene adds hidden, so the GM can show it to the players when they're ready. Quick Start turns it on for a city or metropolis.
- The nobles' palace is drawn as a palace: a walled precinct entered by a gate with a pier each side, a paved court of honor with a fountain between two wings running out from the main range, square pavilions at the corners and a domed one at the middle, and formal gardens behind on the same axis (beds edged with box either side of a broad walk, a basin, rows of trees).
- For plan writers: the top-level `style` (`illustrated` or `textured`) and `output.dressing` (true or false). `checkPlan` warns about dressing that isn't true or false, or asked of a textured map. A layout now carries each district's outline (`district` items), the map's `title` and `dressing`.

**Denser cities**
- A city's busy quarters are built up in continuous rows. Each plot's house takes the plot's whole street front, fitted to it (following a curving street), under a roof whose ridge runs along the street, and the party walls between the houses show. At a corner, or where a plot runs through to a street behind, the row turns and carries on along that street too. A wide row is sometimes built in two sections, the second shallower under a roof of its own. Behind the rows are yards with sheds, gardens and trees, and now and then a back range closing a small court. A busy ward is now about half roofs (it was about a third).
- Homes, market, crafts and poor quarters build in rows, and about half of a merchants' quarter; the nobles, temple, garrison and docks keep their own houses. Towns and villages are not built in rows. A row's roof is a gable, or flat where the house's form would be flat: its ends are party walls, so it takes no hips (a district's own roof forms still apply to its other buildings).
- Deeper houses: a house kept shallow to leave room for wings it then had no room for now takes its plot's full depth, and a house narrows in finer steps before its plot is left as a yard. Most visible in cities; houses everywhere move a little.
- A plan building's shape can now be any polygon (it was drawn as its bounding box): it is drawn under a gable whose ridge runs the way the building is turned. A pinned row house keeps its own outline.
- A district's centerpiece no longer always stands in its middle with every street ringing it and leading out from it. It stands in the middle, off to one side or at the district's edge (often on a main road), picked from the map's seed, and the streets near one off to the side mostly follow the district's main roads. A new Centerpiece place setting picks it for a district. Rerolling a district still leaves its centerpiece where it is.
- A track out to the fields from a lane's end keeps clear of a pinned house there, as it does of the district's own.
- For plan writers: a district's `anchorAt` (`middle`, `side` or `edge`; left out, the plan's seed picks). A building's `shape` may be a list of points, turned by `rot` as any shape is, its ridge along the turn. A row house's layout item carries `poly`, its outline on the map. Every city plan and Quick Start city draws differently, and cached layouts are redrawn.

## 0.5.0

Walled cities. Quick Start makes a city or a metropolis with its wall, gates, wards, harbor, citadel and country around it, and every district can have a character and its centerpiece. Also new: water (ponds, lakes, the sea, quays and piers), town walls, cliffs, more kinds of squares, market stalls, cemeteries, mills and boats.

**Squares, cliffs and city outlines**
- Squares come in more pavings: cobbles, square setts, flagstones, herringbone brick, brick and stone tiles. A poorer place's square is bare ground (dirt, trodden earth or gravel): a hamlet's always, a village's mostly, a town's about half the time, and only a paved place's main roads are cobbled.
- Quick Start squares take more shapes: a circle, a rectangle along the main road, an irregular market place, a triangle where the roads fork, or the main road widened into a long market street. A city's market square is a circle, a rectangle or an irregular place, in any of the pavings.
- Cliffs: a new Cliff tool draws a cliff along its top edge, the drop on its left as it's drawn (its "Drop is on the" setting turns it). Its settings: its height and its rock (earthy brown stone or gray slate). It's painted from Forgotten Adventures' free cliff art (the face, the lip along its top and rubble at its foot; plain stone without the art), and an end inside the map dies away over a few squares. Houses, streets, fields and farm lanes keep off it, and `checkPlan` warns about a road that runs over one.
- A city's outline: a new Outline choice in Quick Start (left on Any, the seed picks one that suits the water): compact, lobed and uneven, on a promontory (the sea or a lake on three sides, the wall across its neck, a harbor all around; its gates all face the neck, so it has one or two), long along its shore or river, or against a cliff (the wall ends at the cliff on each side; the city stands at its foot or on top). Water left on Any is picked to suit the outline (or is a river when you pick a River shape), and a River shape you pick wins over an outline it can't go with.
- Now and then Quick Start puts an escarpment out in the country of a hamlet, village or town, clear of the place, its roads and its farms.
- A triangle square at a fork has a corner between each two roads, so each road leaves through the middle of a side. A city against a cliff gets the gates its Roads choice asks for even by the sea or a lake. A metropolis's old wall is drawn in where it would run along the street inside the town wall.
- A cliff can be clicked anywhere on its face in the window, and a cliff's ends snap onto other cliffs, not onto walls (nor a wall's onto a cliff).
- `checkPlan` warns about a boat or ship drawn on dry land, a setting given to a class that doesn't use it (a cliff's `outside`, say), and a cliff too short to read as one; it no longer stops on a shape with a missing corner.
- In the window: a river's Width or End width changed by hand replaces its widths at each point (a Quick Start city's river has them, so the two settings did nothing); Centerpiece is shown only for a character that has one; a cliff's or wall's corners, and a pond's, lake's or sea's, snap to and move with their own kind only; hints name a feature by its name; Quick Start says when an Outline or River you picked couldn't be made and another was used.
- A city's river around an island where it meets the sea now has the market square on the island (it ran through the square), and a city's river keeps clear of a rectangular square's corners. A city's burial grounds are placed past its suburbs, so most cities get one or two (many got none). Quick Start farms are tested at more points against the roads and water, which moves a farm in a few existing seeds.
- For plan writers: wall class `cliff` with `side` (its low side) and `rock`; surfaces `setts`, `herringbone`, `brick` and `tiles` (for squares, roads and a district's streets). A wall's `height` is now at most 400 feet and a prop's `size` at most 500 (a bigger number stops the render). `checkPlan` warns about a wall that crosses itself, a road running along a wall rather than through it, a boat or ship on dry land, and a sea with nothing left of it on the map. Quick Start plans change: squares, roads' surfaces and city outlines.

**Market places, cemeteries, mills and harbors**
- Market stalls: a square's new Market stalls setting fills it with stalls in rows (awnings of many colors, plain and striped), lanes between and goods set out, clear of the roads through it. Quick Start turns it on for its market square, and a market quarter's square has stalls too.
- A square's new In its middle setting: a fountain or a market cross near its middle, clear of the roads; left unset, its seed picks (a market square mostly a cross, a paved square often a fountain, a green sometimes a cross).
- Cemeteries are drawn: a gate where the nearest road meets one, a path across it (and a cross path in a big one), graves in rows with open patches, yews, tombs beside the paths and a chapel in a big one, and a drystone wall or a hedge around it (its Border setting). A Cemetery tool draws one; out in the country a lane comes to its gate. Quick Start gives a town a cemetery at its edge and a city one or two burial grounds outside its walls.
- Mills: a building used as a windmill gets sails over its cap, and one used as a watermill a wheel on its side facing the water. New Windmill and Watermill tools. Quick Start puts a watermill on a short mill race beside the river by a village or town (sometimes a hamlet) and outside a city's walls, and windmills among the fields.
- Boats and cranes: rowboats moored along quays, wharves, piers and jetties, ships at the sea's quays and piers, cranes on stone quays, a few ships at anchor off a harbor and rowboats on a river by the houses (none on frozen water). Ships are drawn in plain shapes where FA Nexus has no hull art for you. New Boat, Ship, Crane, Fountain and Market cross tools.
- For plan writers: square fields `market` and `middle`; a cemetery's `border`; prop classes `fountain`, `cross`, `boat`, `ship`, `crane`; building uses `chapel`, `tomb`, `windmill`, `watermill`. Quick Start plans change (markets, mills and cemeteries), and every map with quays, piers or a river by its houses now draws boats.

**Quick Start cities**
- Quick Start makes a walled city or a metropolis: a wall with a gate for each main road, the main roads meeting at a central market square, a street inside the wall, and wards (Voronoi cells between the main roads) bounded by streets, each an ordinary district you can reroll, lock and reshape.
- City water: a river through it, a sea coast with a harbor (a quay and piers), a lake shore (a wharf and jetties), or a river meeting the sea.
- A city's river bends through it, splits around an island at its heart, loops around it outside the wall (all but a neck of land), or meets a second river inside the walls. A new River choice picks the shape; left on Any, the seed picks.
- Quick Start rivers and streams in villages, hamlets and towns now bend through the country instead of running nearly straight. Existing Quick Start seeds get different rivers, and the farms and fields around them move with them.
- Wards get characters by the city's geography, varied by the seed: market and temple at the center, docks on the water, the garrison against the wall by a gate (sometimes walled in as a citadel with its own gate), the nobles on the quiet side, crafts and the poor by the wall and downstream, merchants along the main roads, homes in between. Each ward is named (Market Ward, High Ward, North Ward and so on).
- A metropolis keeps its old inner wall around the old town, with more wards, gates and suburbs.
- Outside the walls: suburbs along the roads beyond some gates, farmsteads, fields and woods.
- Every district of type City now builds up its block middles with houses and yards on trodden ground, and its lanes run across its main streets only (except around a radial square), so its blocks are bigger and fuller. Quick Start town cores change with this.
- A city of ten or more districts gives a temple to about a third of its districts and an inn and a smithy to about half, not one of each to every district.
- A street's seeds along a road on the district's edge now start just inside it, so districts bounded by roads (a city's wards) fill with streets.
- For a city, the Roads choice sets the gates (a road through: 2, a fork: 3, a crossroads: 4; a metropolis 2 more), and a village green makes a grass square where the main roads meet.
- `checkPlan` no longer reports two bent districts that only share a side as overlapping.
- A track doesn't start from a lane's end beside a house (it would clip it).

**District character and what buildings are for**
- A district's Character: homes (the default, which changes nothing), market, merchants, crafts, docks, nobles, temple, poor quarter or garrison. It shifts the district's plots, house shapes, gardens, roofs and streets: narrow packed plots and crooked lanes in the poor quarter, big houses set back in walled grounds for the nobles, courtyard houses and warehouses for the merchants, barracks and straight streets for the garrison.
- Each character reads apart at a glance: its own ground (cobbles in the market, merchants' and docks' quarters, bare dirt in the crafts and poor quarters, trodden earth for the garrison, lawns for the nobles and the temple), its own street surface, its own roof colors, and in the dense quarters the middles of the blocks built up with stores, workshops or shacks.
- Each character has its centerpiece on its own square, placed before the streets, which lead to it and run around it, with the houses about it facing it: a cathedral (nave, transepts, a round east end, two towers, a cloister) before a paved square with a fountain; the nobles' palace with a domed pavilion, a gravel forecourt and a formal garden; the market square with its hall, a fountain and a statue; a guildhall on its square or work yard; the docks' customs house with cranes; the garrison's keep on its parade ground; the poor quarter's shrine and well. Centerpieces are drawn larger than life, the cathedral and the palace with green copper roofs that catch the light. A district's Centerpiece setting turns it off; a centerpiece can be clicked but not pinned.
- Every building the generator makes has a use (house, shop, inn, temple, mansion, warehouse, barracks, stable, a trade), shown when you click it and kept when you pin it. A building you draw can be given one.
- Villages and bigger get notable buildings of their own: a temple near the green or square, an inn on a road through, a smithy toward the edge. Notable buildings (on by default) turns them off for a district. Existing plans change a little where these now stand.
- Five uses have a building of their own: a temple (nave, chancel, tower, transepts when big) in a walled yard, a mansion in walled grounds with a gravel drive and an avenue, a long warehouse, barracks around a yard, and an inn with a stable across its yard.
- Trades, each placed by its own rule: smiths and farriers on the roads and by the gates (no thatch on them or their neighbors), tanners, dyers and fullers by the water and downstream, potters and brickmakers toward the edge with kilns, carpenters, coopers, wheelwrights, bakers, brewers, butchers, alchemists, magic shops, laboratories, gunsmiths and inventors. Their yards show their work: crates, barrels, timber, hay, carts, racks, colored dyeing pits, kilns, and smoke drifting with the wind.
- Street corners get the quarter's wells, fountains, shrines and watch posts.
- Make scene adds a small hidden label on each notable building with its use (a centerpiece's as "Palace", "Market hall" and so on), or the name you gave it; where two would overlap, the more telling one stays, and none is placed off the map or over a plan's label.
- A temple's walled yard has a few graves.
- For plan writers: district fields `character`, `trades` (a list), `notable` and `anchor`; building fields `use` (with `palace` and `market-hall`), `tower` and `crenels` (a pinned watch post keeps its battlemented tower). `checkPlan` now reads the settings of a landmark's buildings as it does a building's. An old plan's `gardens: true` now means its character's share of gardens. A river flows from its first point to its last.

**Water**
- Ponds, lakes and the sea, drawn as shapes with three new tools (Pond, Lake, Sea; a box dragged with the Pond or Lake tool is the oval inside it). A sea's corners near the map's edge go past it, so its outline is the coast. Shores are curved and a little ragged.
- Shores: sand, pebbles, rocks, grass, marsh or reeds, picked from the seed unless set (a pond's is grass or reeds; a sea's sand, pebbles or rocks). The water is lighter in the shallows and darker farther out, the sea has surf along its coast outside winter, reedy and marshy shores have reeds, and still ponds and lakes have lily pads outside winter.
- In winter ponds and lakes freeze unless their Frozen setting says otherwise; the sea doesn't, but has ice along its shore.
- Rivers, streams and canals can widen or narrow: End width is the width at the last point, and the width changes smoothly along the way. A river opens out where it meets a lake or the sea, and flows in with no bank across its mouth.
- Quays and piers: two new tools. A quay (stone) or wharf (timber) is drawn along the water's edge; its line is moved onto the nearest edge of a river, lake or the sea (onto the bank most of it is on, or a town wall's moat), it's paved back from the water, and houses face it as they would a street; one out in open water isn't drawn, and one moved out of the district it was drawn in says so. A pier (stone) or jetty (timber) runs out into the water as drawn.
- Houses, streets, trees and fields keep off ponds, lakes, the sea and their shores, and a town wall stops at a lake's shore as it does at a river.
- For plan writers: water classes `pond`, `lake` and `sea` with a `shape`; water fields `endWidth`, `widths` (a width in feet for each point of the path, in place of `width` and `endWidth`) and `shore`; a new feature type `dock` (classes `quay`, `wharf`, `pier`, `jetty`; fields `width` and `material`, stone or wood). `checkPlan` warns about a pond, lake or sea drawn along a path, a river drawn as a shape, and `widths` that don't match the path.

**Fixes**
- A farm's lane never runs through a town wall (only roads get gates), and a park's lane never starts in a river.
- Fields keep clear of the trees, wells and carts a plan places. A plan tree's species can be any conifer's name (pine, fir, spruce and so on), and a prop's Size (in feet) now sets how big it's drawn.
- A village green stands out from the fields around it in summer.
- Labels: a name label on a turned area turns with it; a label's angle can be any number of degrees (370 reads as 10).
- In the window: a Stop button while a slow map draws; a click on a label's text picks the label rather than a well or cart under it; hints name districts by name; a building group whose roofs differ shows "mixed" rather than a Default that did nothing; the underlay's see-through slider no longer jumps back when the map finishes drawing; a scene publish canceled after the map was saved says where the map is.
- Publishing into a scene replaces every label AtlasScribe put there before, whatever plan made them, and a canceled publish no longer leaves a new thumbnail behind.
- Cities: a ward too narrow for its centerpiece along its main road takes it turned a quarter, or smaller (down to about half size), so far fewer wards go without one; a centerpiece beside a slanting road is fitted more finely, which moves it a little on some maps. A notable building that finds no plot deep enough tries the district's shallower plots (a city's are shallow), and an inn with still no room for a building of its own takes over a house on its best plot, so towns and cities that missed an inn now have one. A citadel's wall keeps off the market square. A city's village green (and any square given a grass surface) is painted as grass; it was a plain pale fill. A Quick Start city where a river splits around an island near the sea keeps its harbor inside the wall more often, and has its docks on the river when the sea lies beyond it. A plot that wraps around another plot is left for its street, so plots no longer overlap there.
- A district or cemetery whose shape has no area no longer stops the map; the window's progress bar moves through the districts of a big city.

**Lighter lines**
- Roads no longer have a dark line down the middle (ruts are unchanged), and their worn middles, trodden edges, the outlines around roofs and the dark edge along water are lighter.

**Town walls**
- Town walls and palisades: two new drawing tools (click a wall's first point again to close it around), and Wall in on a district's panel for a town wall around it. A gate wherever a road crosses (a gatehouse over the road, or a tower each side), an opening where water crosses, and towers at the corners and about every 120 feet (90 for a palisade).
- Settings: thickness, height, towers (roofed, open with battlements, or some of each), tower shape (round, square or some of each), tower spacing, and what runs along the outside (a dry ditch or a moat, with bridges where the roads cross). A wall picks its own look from its seed unless set.
- A clear strip along each wall: houses keep off it, districts' streets stop at the wall, and only roads pass, through its gates. Fields and woods keep clear too.
- Where a wall meets water it stops at a tower on the bank. A moat runs on into a river it meets, so where the river runs close along the wall the water comes right up to it; a dry ditch stops short of the water.
- Water is painted banks first, so where a stream or a moat opens into a river no bank line runs across its mouth (this changes how stream and river junctions look on existing maps).
- For plan writers: wall classes `town` and `palisade`; fields `thickness`, `towers`, `towerShape`, `towerSpacing`, `outside`, `ditchWidth` and `side` (an open run's outside, left or right going along it). A wall whose last point is its first is closed. `checkPlan` warns about a closed wall no road crosses (it has no gate).

**Faster on big maps**
- Big maps lay out several times faster (the speed-up itself changes no map). A town scaled up to 200 by 152 squares takes about 1.5 seconds instead of about 6, four towns side by side on a 200 by 200 map about 2.5 instead of about 7, and a 400 by 304 map under 10 instead of over a minute. Painting the image is unchanged: on a big map it's most of the wait (about 25 seconds for 200 by 152 squares at 50 pixels a square).

## 0.4.0

Editing on the map: in the AtlasScribe window you can now change any feature's settings, draw new districts, areas, roads, rivers, walls, buildings, props and labels, place and turn labels, pin houses and streets the generator made, and trace over an image of your own, with faster redraws.

**In the window**
- Settings: click anything on the map, or a district or area in the list, and its settings open in the side panel. Only the settings it uses are shown, each with what applies when it's left empty; a change redraws the map at once, and the arrow beside a setting puts it back to its default. Less used settings are under More. Delete takes a feature out (Undo brings it back). The gear opens the plan's own settings: name, season, ground, the map's size, feet and pixels a square, the seed, the labels' color and font, and the scene's settings.
- Draw: a palette of tools (district, woods, fields, park, farm, yard, square, building; road, lane, track, river, stream, wall, hedge; bridge, ford, tree, well, cart, label). Shapes are drawn corner by corner or as a box and snap onto other shapes' corners and sides and the map's edge; where a new district meets another along a side they share that side, so their streets meet across it. Roads, rivers and walls are drawn point by point and snap onto their own kind or the map's edge. Double-click, Enter, Finish or a click on the first corner ends a drawing; Backspace or Delete takes the last point off; Esc cancels.
- Bridges and lanes come with what you draw: a new road over water (or water over a road) gets a bridge, or a ford for a track or path over a stream; a district with no way in gets a lane from the nearest road, and a square off the roads gets a street; farms, yards, parks and squares out in the country get a faint lane to the nearest road (farms used to only within 12 squares, now within 40). These are ordinary features you can change or delete.
- Labels: drawn at their true size, in their color, font and opacity. Drag a label to move it, or the dot beside it to turn it (Shift for steps of 15 degrees). Dropped on a road, river or stream it turns to run along it (not inside a square). A district or area can show its name on the map ("Name on the map"), and the label follows it when it's reshaped. Labels never redraw the map, and scenes get their turn, color, font and opacity.
- Pin: click a house or a street the generator made, and Pin copies it into the plan, so rerolls keep it. A pinned house can be named, labeled and given a new roof; a pinned street becomes a plan road along the same line. The district rebuilds around what's pinned when it's rerolled.
- Underlay: an image under the sketch to trace over (a hand sketch, an old map). Drag it to move it, drag a corner to scale it, or type where it sits; set its opacity, hide it or lock it. It's saved with the plan and never painted into a scene.
- Faster: after a change, only the districts the change reaches are laid out again, and the art FA Nexus found is remembered until Foundry reloads, so an Update scene on a town takes about 3 seconds instead of about 8.

**Woods and districts**
- A wood's density sets its character, so it keeps its look at any size: sparse (lone trees and small clumps), open (big trees standing apart, like wood pasture), thick (groves with clearings, the default) or dense (crowns touching). Setting a density clears a tree count, and the other way around. Trees are bigger, and woods are mostly leafy outside winter and mostly firs in winter.
- The district type `core` is now `city` (plans that say `core` still work). Districts and areas can have a name, shown in the window in place of their id.
- `checkPlan` no longer warns that a district has no road into it when a neighboring district's streets reach it.

**Maps that will look different after updating**
- Woods redraw with the new trees; a district's streets now stop short of plan buildings rather than run into them (and no longer steer around them), so districts with buildings of their own may lay out a little differently; farms farther from a road now get a lane. Reroll or lock as you like.

**For plan writers**
- New plan fields: `underlay`; a label's `angle`, `color`, `font`, `alpha`, `of` (a district's, area's or landmark's id, in place of `at`) and `offset`; a district's or area's `name`; a wood's `density`; `output.scene.labelColor` and `labelFont`; buildings' `chimneyAt`, `join` and `cut` (from pinning). `quickPlan`'s choice `centre` is now `center` (the old name still works).
- American spelling throughout. The field art role `field.ploughed` is now `field.plowed`.

## 0.3.0

Quick Start: a hamlet, village or town from a few choices in the AtlasScribe window (a button in the Scenes tab), changed in a quick preview and made into a scene, with generated streets, plots, houses, fields and woods.

- The AtlasScribe window, from a button in the Scenes tab: Quick Start (a hamlet, village or town from a few choices: season, water, roads, a square or a green, and farms and woods around it; any left to the seed), the preview with rerolls, locks and reshaping, the plan's problems, saving plans in the world (the output folder's `plans` folder; the last one opens with the window), opening, importing and exporting them, and Make scene or Update scene. `api.open()` opens it; `api.quickPlan(choices)` makes a plan without it.
- Preview: `api.preview(plan)` opens a window with a quick sketch of the plan in plain colors, laid out and drawn in about a second with no art, its districts and areas outlined and named. Reroll any district or area there, lock the ones you like, reroll the rest, undo, then copy the plan or take it from the window (`app.plan`) to render. Click a district, area, road, river or wall (on the map, or a name in the list) to show its handles, or turn on Shapes to show them all; drag a district's or area's corners to reshape it, or a road's, river's or wall's points to move it (a point where two roads meet moves for both, and bridges and fords follow to where the roads now cross the water) (a corner two districts share moves for both, and a corner dropped on another snaps to it), drag the dot in the middle of a side to add a corner (on a side two districts share, both get it), and alt-click or right-click a corner to remove it; a reshape that would make a district cross itself is refused; a circle's or ellipse's edge handle sets its size. The map redraws when you let go.
- Streets: districts now make their own streets and lanes, steered by a tensor field built from the plan's roads, rivers and squares, so they run along and across the roads and bend gently. A square with `radial: true` gets streets that ring it. District types (the district's `profile`): `hamlet` (no new streets), `village`, `town` and `core`, each with its own spacing, bend and lanes; `bend`, `spacing` and `axis` override them. Where two districts meet, their streets meet at fixed points on the shared edge, so rerolling one never moves the other. Streets cross water where it makes sense and place their own bridges and footbridges.
- Plots: houses now stand on plots cut from the blocks between streets (after Vanegas et al. 2012, the method CityEngine uses, and the burgage and toft-and-croft plots of real medieval towns and villages): a band along each street side, cut square to the street at varied widths, the wider street taking the corner, plots meeting back to back halfway to the next street. The house stands at the front (set back a little in villages, on the street line in towns and cores, where neighbors share walls), with a shed, barn or workshop behind by district type, and in hamlets and villages sometimes a croft (a small field) behind the yard: about a quarter of village plots, half way back, fenced along its sides and open at the back so it fades into the fields. A district's `crofts` (share), `croftDepth` (0.1 to 1) and `croftFence` (`all`, `sides`, `none`) change that. No plot lines are drawn by default: plots show by what stands and grows on them. A district's `plotLines: true` draws them: in hamlets and villages a faint fence, low wall or hedge around each house's yard and around a croft (never along a street), elsewhere faint walls between long plots behind the yards. Sheds and barns are small and stand across the yard, so they never read as a second house. A district's `spacious` (0 to 1) widens plots and spaces houses out. Uses Clipper2 (Boost license), bundled in `vendor/`.
- Building shapes: houses take the forms medieval town houses took on their plots: a plain block, a rear wing running back from one end (an L), two wings (a U), or, on a deep, wide plot, wings and a back range around a yard (a courtyard house or inn). Town and core plots are now and then joined into a wealthier double plot, whose house leans to the bigger forms. In towns and cores, houses in a row share a roof material, so a terrace reads as a row. Roofs are painted as real roofs: hipped (four slopes, hips to the corners) or gabled, each slope textured along its eave and shaded by which way it faces the sun; a wing's roof runs on into its house's through a pair of valleys, with no outline across the join. Village and hamlet houses are mostly hipped; terraces and sheds are gabled; a plan building can say `roofStyle`.
- Back lanes: towns now follow the burgage pattern of real medieval towns: main streets wider apart, long narrow plots running from the street back to a back lane a plot's depth behind it, a few cross lanes, and at the far end of many plots a stable, workshop or warehouse on the lane. Back lanes carry no house fronts; where one would run into a neighboring district it turns back to its own street. Corner plots on curving streets now go to the street they actually face, so cores and towns have fewer empty plots.
- Filling the open ground: the back of each plot, behind the yard, now holds a strip garden, an orchard in rows, a few trees, or open ground, by district type, and the ground no plot takes (beyond the back lanes, the middle of big blocks) gets trees scattered evenly in loose clumps (Poisson-disk sampling, thinned by a noise). Long town plots show as strips through what's in them.
- Roof forms (from the roofs in hand-drawn town maps): besides gabled and hipped roofs, houses now get half-hips (a gable with its peak clipped into a small hip), flat roofs behind a stone parapet, and in cores mansards (steep slopes around a flat top with a glass skylight). Square sheds get pyramid roofs, and now and then a shed is round, a dovecote under a cone. Ridges and hips carry a pale ridge cap, so each roof's shape reads at a glance; in winter the caps are snowy. Every roof's texture now runs downslope on each slope.
- House plans and details: besides L, U and courtyard houses, some houses have a wing from the middle of the back (a T). A house set back from the street sometimes has a bay out to the front under its own small roof (a cross-gable); a long roof sometimes has gables on its street side, wall dormers or cross-gables rising straight from the eave; a long plain house is often built in two or three sections, each with its own roof, stepping in depth and height; and a few big houses have a round corner turret under a cone. The details never change where houses stand.
- Roof colors: roofs now come in the colors of the art, not just plain slate, shingle and red or brown tile: black, blue, purple and mossy slate; red, orange, brown, tan, gray, black, blue, green, purple and mossy tile in several tile shapes; dark, light, red, ashen and walnut shingle; and thatch. Each district keeps a short palette of its own, picked from its seed by type (thatch and shingle in hamlets, slate in villages, colored slate and tile in towns), so neighboring districts can look different. A district's `roofs` and `roofStyles` (lists) set its own. In winter, colored roofs get snow on them too.
- `checkPlan` warns when a district's or area's outline crosses itself.
- Fixes: a shape less than a tenth of a square across (an area, square, building or district) is now reported by `checkPlan` instead of crashing the layout; houses and their parts never reach into a street, their own district's or a neighbor's.
- Woods grow in groves: trees close enough that their crowns touch, gathered where a noise runs high, with open ground and clearings between, holding about as many trees as the area asks for (`trees`). A building or road placed later removes only the trees it stands on.
- Parks: an area of class `park` is now drawn, by its `style`. A village green (`green`, the default): open grass, big trees in groups around the edge and a few in the open, and trodden paths from the middle of each side a road runs along to a point near the middle. A formal garden (`formal`), laid out square to the area's `rot`: gravel walks around it and across it, a round basin where they cross, four beds with clipped hedges, and avenues of trees along the long walk. Streets and plots keep out of parks.
- Rerolling and locking: `api.reroll(plan, ids)` gives a plan with new seeds for those districts or areas (or every one that isn't locked), so they're drawn again while everything else stays exactly as it was; `api.lock(plan, ids)` and `api.unlock(plan, ids)` mark the ones a reroll must leave alone. Rerolling is repeatable: the same plan rerolls the same way, and rerolling the result gives the next variation.
- Curves: roads, rivers and walls now curve smoothly through the plan's points instead of running straight between them, so a point placed by hand stays on the line and a road that ended on another still meets it. A sharp turn (over 60 degrees) on a road or river is rounded off instead, so it never swings wide of its line; a ring road or a wall around a yard curves all the way around. Walls keep their sharp corners and curve only their gentle bends. Where a curve would swing into a building the plan placed, the notes say so. `straight: true` on a road, water or wall keeps it straight (a canal, a Roman road, a town wall's runs). Districts' streets follow the curves, so a plan's districts lay out a little differently than before.
- Bridges and fords line up with their roads: a plan's crossing is moved to the nearest place a road crosses the water (each place takes one crossing), turned to run along the road, made long enough to span the water and its banks at that angle, and a bridge made as wide as its road. One with no road over water near it stays where the plan puts it, with a note.
- Summer art: maps outside winter now paint with FA Nexus art too (they were mostly plain color): grass with lighter, lusher and patchy grass in patches; dirt roads and grassy verges and yards; gravel, cobbles and stone; river, stream and pond water; leafy trees, green firs in three sizes and green hedge bushes, with yellow, red and russet trees and bushes in autumn. Spring and autumn use the summer art unless they have their own. Outside winter the grass is lifted toward a lighter green and the darkening at the map's edges is halved, since FA's grass reads dark at overview scale. Winter dirt roads are slush. Deciduous trees are drawn in leaf outside winter (bare only in winter).
- Field systems: an area of class `fields` is now drawn, filled with fields from a Voronoi diagram (d3-delaunay, ISC license, bundled in `vendor/`). Two systems, by the area's `system` (else picked from its seed): `patchwork`, enclosed fields of irregular shape running long with the lie of the area, a few merged into bigger ones, hedged (with a tree in the hedge here and there) or walled along the roads, around the system's edge and on about a quarter of the sides between fields, the rest a faint grass edge, so not every field is ringed; and `strips`, open fields in furlongs, each plowed in long narrow strips running with the land or across it, with grass balks between. About a third of the fields (or whole furlongs) are left as grass, pasture or common, so the land isn't cultivated wall to wall; `coverage` (0 to 1) sets the share that's cultivated. `border` (`hedge`, `drystone` or `none`) and `fieldSize` (about how big a field or furlong is, in feet) change them too. Each field has a crop for the season (plowed, growing, ripe, stubble, hay, pasture or fallow), painted with FA Nexus crop, hay, clover and soil art and drawn with its rows (furrows, drills, the windrows of cut hay). Fields keep off the roads, water and walls, a ragged margin of grass 75 to 200 feet wide around every district (so a village sits in its own grass rather than as a box among the crops) and 30 to 90 feet around every farmyard, a farm's lane (a track from each farmstead to the road beside it), squares, yards, buildings, other areas and any field system listed before them (where two systems meet, only the first draws a border along the edge). They come from the area's own shape and seed, so moving a road clips them differently but never reshuffles them (strips, hedgerow trees and all), and rerolling a field system redraws it alone. Scraps and slivers the roads and margins cut off are left as grass, with no balks beside them. Under snow a field is mostly the snow around it, its own texture showing only faintly. A huge area with a small `fieldSize` gets bigger fields, with a note, rather than running out of fields partway across.
- Quick Start's farmland is now field systems in the stretches of land between the roads (all but one stretch in farmland, running to the map's edge; about half in mixed country, a ragged way out from the village; now and then in woodland), in place of the field walls along the roads.
- Tracks: a district's street that stops at the district's edge now carries on as a narrow trodden track out into the land, wandering a little, until it reaches a field's gate, a road, water, a wood or other area, or its length, so streets no longer end abruptly at the edge. These tracks and the farm lanes are drawn faint; a road of class `track` in the plan is drawn as a road.
- Squares sit in their roads rather than on top of them: a worn apron around each that the roads and streets run into, the paving laid with a soft, worn edge, and the road's worn line carried faintly across it. A back lane that has just crossed a street now ends there, instead of running on and laying a link back to its own street alongside that one.
- Gardens: only a share of houses get a tended garden (the district's `gardens`, a share from 0 to 1, about one in ten by default; `true` and `false` from older plans still read), in varied sizes, and they're drawn quietly: a thin fence and faint rows in winter, soft planted rows in other seasons.
- Make scene saves the plan first (so the scene can always be updated from it later), has a Cancel button while it paints, and says when art couldn't be had and parts are painted in plain colors. A plan with no id gets one from its name with a short random tail, so two plans of the same name never share a file or a scene.
- Winter: a gravel square or road under snow is trodden slush, not near-black gravel.
- Plans: `checkPlan` now stops a plan that would take minutes to lay out (a feature reaching far past the map, or more than 100 feet a square, or a plan seed that isn't a number), reads a building's `variant`, warns about a feature's seed that isn't a number, no longer calls an outline that crosses itself too small, and says plainly that where districts overlap, both build and their houses stand on each other's. A wood with `trees: 0` is open ground, and one too big to fill says so. A district's or area's outline lays out the same whichever way around its corners run. Houses keep off a road running just outside their district, a back lane never links across into the next district, and no green's tree or neighbor's tree stands in a street. Laying out a big town is faster.

## 0.2.0

The engine: a map plan in, a painted map and a scene out, driven from the console (see the README's "From a script").

- Plans: `api.checkPlan(plan)` reads a plan (JSON, or a fenced `mapplan` block) and checks it, explaining each problem in plain words, errors first; a badly formed plan is reported, never crashes it. One list of feature types (roads, water, crossings, squares, buildings, landmarks, districts, areas, walls, props and labels) sets what each takes and its defaults; the image settings (`output`) and the scene settings (`output.scene`) are checked too, including Foundry's smallest grid and the largest image WebP can hold.
- Rendering: `api.render(plan)` lays the plan out and paints it in a background worker, so Foundry never freezes, with a progress bar, then saves the image without writing over anything. The first fill is the "roadside" district (houses along every road and lane through it, each with a garden behind; houses and gardens never overlap each other, a yard, a square, water or a road), plus farmsteads, woods of any shape, hedgerows and a textured winter style. Art comes from FA Nexus when it's installed, through a role map; anything missing, or everything if FA Nexus can't be used, is painted in plain colors. Big maps paint in tiles, with no visible joins.
- Seeds: the same plan always gives the same map, and each district, farm and wood has its own seed (`seeds`): rerolling one district never moves the district beside it.
- Publishing: `api.publish(plan, { image })`, or `api.render(plan, { publish: true })`, makes the map a scene: the background, the grid, lighting, fog, weather and navigation from `output.scene`, a thumbnail, and the plan's labels as locked text drawings (hidden from players unless a label says `show: "all"`; sizes small, normal, large and huge). Give `scene: <id>` to update a scene AtlasScribe made (any other needs `force: true`): the whole scene is backed up first, its name and your own drawings are kept, and only its old labels are replaced.
- `api.cancel()` stops the job that's running; one job runs at a time.
